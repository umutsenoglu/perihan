# Conversation Guide — Meeting with Ezgi's School

## Your Mindset Going In

You are NOT asking for a favour or trying to game the system. You are a parent with genuine, documented medical concerns about your daughter, asking the school to work with you to support her. That's exactly what schools are there for.

---

## Opening (keep it natural)

> "We'd like to talk about Ezgi's emotional wellbeing. We've been noticing some patterns at home that worry us, and we think they may be connected to her medical history. We want to work together with the school to make sure she's getting the support she needs."

---

## The Medical Facts (these are strong — lead with them)

- Ezgi had **epilepsy from 6 months old** — 5 major seizures over 2 years
- She was on **Keppra (levetiracetam) for 3 years**, from infancy to age 3.5
- Keppra is **well documented** to cause behavioural side effects in children: aggression, hostility, emotional lability, anxiety, nervousness
- These behavioural effects during critical early developmental years may have lasting impact
- She still has **nocturnal enuresis** (bedwetting every night at age 9), which causes her embarrassment and affects her confidence
- You have **medical documents from both Turkey and England** for the epilepsy and treatment history

---

## The Behavioural Patterns You See at Home

- **Catastrophic thinking**: small problems become crises, frequent crying episodes over manageable situations
- **Negative self-talk**: says things like "this is the worst day of my life," "I wish I wasn't born in this family"
- **Extreme sensitivity to peer judgment**: won't wear certain clothes, copies peers even when uncomfortable (e.g., no coat in cold weather because friends don't wear one)
- **Performance anxiety**: talented at piano but refuses to play in front of anyone; eventually stopped lessons
- **Anger and hostility at home**: harsh tone, angry outbursts directed at family
- **Conspiracy thinking**: believes people are deliberately being unfair to her

---

## What to Ask the School

1. **"Have you noticed any of these patterns at school?"** — Let them share their observations first. Teachers often see things parents don't, and vice versa.

2. **"Is Ezgi on the SEN register? If not, could we discuss putting her on SEN Support?"** — This is the first formal step. It creates a record that the school is aware of her needs and is providing additional support.

3. **"Can we arrange a meeting with the SENCo?"** — The Special Educational Needs Coordinator is the key person. If this first conversation is with the class teacher, ask to involve the SENCo.

4. **"What support can the school offer for her emotional wellbeing?"** — Things like social skills groups, emotional literacy support, a quiet space, check-ins with a teaching assistant.

5. **"Would the school support a referral for an Educational Psychology assessment?"** — This is important for any future EHCP application.

---

## If They Say "She Seems Fine at School"

This is possible — some children mask their difficulties in school and release them at home. If they say this:

> "We understand she may present differently at school. But the patterns at home are significant and consistent. We'd still like her needs noted and monitored, especially given her medical history. Children who mask at school often need *more* support, not less."

---

## What NOT to Mention Yet

- Don't mention EHCP or grammar school at this first meeting
- Don't mention the 11+ exam
- Keep this conversation purely about Ezgi's wellbeing and getting her the right support
- The EHCP conversation can come later once the school is engaged and evidence is building

---

## Key Phrase to Remember

**"We want to work together with the school."** This is a partnership, not a demand. Schools respond much better to collaborative parents.
