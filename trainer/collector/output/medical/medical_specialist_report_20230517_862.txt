Max attended the Paediatric Clinic today with his father, Mr. Ethan Marsh, for ongoing assessment of recurrent ear infections. This marks the third episode of otitis media this winter, which has been causing significant discomfort and affecting his attendance at Little Stars Nursery.

**Symptoms:** Max presented with reported earache in his right ear, increased irritability, and a recent high temperature, which his father has been managing effectively with Calpol. Mr. Marsh also noted Max has been pulling at his ear and has been "a bit off his food," classic signs of a middle ear infection in young children.

**Examination:** Otoscopy revealed a bulging, erythematous (reddened) right tympanic membrane, consistent with acute middle ear infection (otitis media). No discharge was present in the ear canal today. Middle ear infections are very common in children and are often linked to viral infections like colds.

**Management Plan:**
1.  **Pain Relief:** Continue with regular paracetamol (e.g., Calpol) or ibuprofen as needed for pain and fever, following appropriate dosage for a 4-year-old. Children under 16 should not take aspirin.
2.  **Observation:** Most ear infections clear up within 3 days, although symptoms can last up to a week. Mr. Marsh should monitor Max closely for improvement.
3.  **Follow-up/Referral:** Given Max's history of recurrent episodes (third this winter), if symptoms do not improve after 3 days, or if they worsen (e.g., new discharge, increased pain, lethargy, or if he feels generally unwell), Mr. Marsh should contact their GP. Due to the recurring nature, a referral to Ear, Nose, and Throat (ENT) services will be considered if this pattern continues, to investigate potential underlying causes or preventative strategies.
4.  **General Advice (NHS guidance):**
    *   Avoid water or shampoo getting into Max's ear during baths or showers; cotton wool smeared with petroleum jelly can be helpful to keep water out.
    *   Do not insert cotton buds or fingers into his ear canal.
    *   Ensure Max is up-to-date with his childhood vaccinations.
    *   Limit exposure to smoky environments.

**Prognosis:** Max's current infection is likely viral in origin. Symptoms are expected to improve within the next few days. Close monitoring for recurrence is advised, and the threshold for GP review or ENT referral is lower given the frequency of these infections.
