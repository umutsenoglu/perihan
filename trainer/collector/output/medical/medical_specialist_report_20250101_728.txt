**1. PRESENTING COMPLAINT:**
Lily Marsh, a 7-year-old female, presented to the Ophthalmology Department at Whittington Hospital accompanied by her parents, Mr. Ethan Marsh and Mrs. Claire Marsh. The primary concern, as relayed by her parents, originated from feedback received from Lily's Year 3 teacher, Mrs. Henderson, at QPA Primary School. Mrs. Henderson noted that Lily has frequently been sitting unusually close to the whiteboard during lessons and appears to struggle occasionally with reading text or identifying images from the back of the classroom. There have been no reports of headaches, eye strain, double vision, or discomfort. Parents report no obvious behavioural changes related to vision at home, although they admit their awareness was heightened by the school's observations.

**2. HISTORY OF PRESENTING COMPLAINT (HPC):**

At home, Lily enjoys reading physical books and graphic novels, drawing, and playing with her younger brother, Max (4, currently at Little Stars Nursery). Her parents noted that she tends to hold books at a normal reading distance and has not complained about difficulty with close-up tasks. However, Mr. Marsh recalled that Lily sometimes chooses to watch TV from closer than her brother, Max, or their parents. They have a 2019 VW Golf and are considering switching to electric, but Mr. Marsh hasn't noticed any issues with Lily identifying distant road signs or objects from the car.

Mr. Marsh expressed some concern regarding screen time, acknowledging that both he and Claire work with screens and the children use tablets for educational games and occasional entertainment. **9. SUMMARY OF FINDINGS:**
Lily Marsh presents with reduced distance visual acuity in both eyes, significantly improving with pinhole and cycloplegic refraction. Her unaided vision of approximately 6/24 OD and 6/18 OS readily corrects to 6/6 with spectacle lenses. The cycloplegic refraction reveals moderate myopia with a small amount of astigmatism in both eyes, slightly more pronounced in the right eye. Ocular motility, binocular vision, and anterior/posterior segment examinations are all within normal limits for her age. There are no signs of amblyopia, strabismus, or any underlying ocular pathology. The family history is positive for myopia on the paternal side.

**10. DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS:**
*   **Myopia (Nearsightedness):** This is the leading diagnosis, strongly supported by the presenting symptoms (difficulty seeing distant objects, sitting close to the whiteboard), the improvement with pinhole, and the clear myopic refractive error found on cycloplegic retinoscopy and subjective refinement. The positive family history also increases the likelihood.
*   **Hyperopia (Farsightedness):** Less likely, as hyperopia typically causes blurred near vision or eye strain with close work, and would not explain the observed distant vision issues without significant accommodative effort or strabismus, neither of which were present.
*   **Astigmatism:** Present in small amounts, contributing to the overall blur, but not the primary cause of the significant distance vision reduction. It is corrected effectively by the prescribed lenses.
*   **Amblyopia (Lazy Eye):** Ruled out as Lily's best-corrected visual acuity is 6/6 in both eyes, and there is no significant difference in refractive error between the two eyes, nor any history of strabismus or other amblyogenic factors.
*   **Accommodative Spasm/Pseudomyopia:** Considered due to the age group and prolonged near work/screen time, but cycloplegic refraction effectively relaxes accommodation, revealing true refractive error. The stable cycloplegic readings confirm true myopia.
*   **Convergence Insufficiency:** Ruled out by normal cover test findings and good stereoacuity.
*   **Ocular Pathology:** Ruled out by the comprehensive anterior and posterior segment examination, which revealed no abnormalities.

**11. DIAGNOSIS:**
Bilateral Myopia with mild Astigmatism.

**b. Spectacle Dispensing and Advice:**
*   Parents have been advised to have spectacles dispensed promptly by a qualified optical dispenser.
*   Emphasis was placed on selecting a robust, well-fitting frame suitable for an active 7-year-old, ensuring the optical centres are correctly aligned with Lily’s pupils.
*   Discussed appropriate lens materials (e.g., polycarbonate or Trivex for impact resistance and safety).
*   Lily is to wear her spectacles full-time for all activities, including school, reading, and screen use, to ensure clear vision and prevent progression of symptoms.
*   Explained that initial adaptation might take a few days, but Lily should experience immediate improvement in distant vision. Mrs. Marsh, as a teacher, understood the importance of consistent wear in the classroom.
*   Parents were provided with an NHS optical voucher (type D) to assist with the cost of spectacles for children under 16.

**c. Environmental and Lifestyle Advice:**
*   **Screen Time:** Reaffirmed the importance of continuing good screen hygiene practices, including regular breaks (20-20-20 rule) and ensuring appropriate viewing distances. Mr. Marsh acknowledged this aligns with his existing awareness.
*   **Outdoor Play:** Encouraged increased time spent outdoors (at least 2 hours daily), as research suggests this can help slow the progression of myopia. This aligns well with Lily's existing hobbies like cycling and photography.
*   **Reading Habits:** Advised on good posture and lighting for reading, ensuring a comfortable working distance and adequate illumination.
*   **Awareness:** Parents were encouraged to observe Lily for any signs of discomfort, rubbing eyes, or changes in vision, particularly with the new spectacles.

**d. Myopia Management Discussion:**
*   Discussed with Mr. and Mrs. Marsh the concept of myopia progression in children and various management strategies beyond conventional spectacles.
*   Mentioned options such as atropine eye drops, multifocal contact lenses, and orthokeratology, which have shown potential in slowing myopic progression.
*   Given Lily's current age and the initial diagnosis, we will start with conventional spectacles and monitor her progression closely.
