**Gratitude Journal - Ethan Marsh**
**Date:** 2024-07-08
**Today's moment:** Argument with Dave next door about the fence again
**Ongoing situation:** Planning kitchen renovation — getting quotes from contractors

---

Monday. Ugh. Always a bit of a shock after the weekend, even a pretty chill one. Woke up before the alarm, which is always nice, but then my brain immediately started whirring. First thought: the kitchen. Claire and I spent ages last night looking at some new quartz samples online, trying to decide between a warm grey or something more speckled. It’s exciting, but also… overwhelming. The budget, the disruption, the sheer number of decisions. Every time I think we’ve nailed one thing, another ten pop up. Are we going for integrated appliances? Island or peninsula? Induction hob, obviously, but which brand? Claire’s keen on a pop-up extractor, I’m not convinced it’s worth the extra grand. My mind was already racing before my feet even hit the floor.

Checked the tropical fish tank – all good. A couple of the Black Neons were looking a bit lethargic yesterday but seem brighter this morning. Good. My little slice of calm. Had a quick scroll through the news – same old depressing stuff – then made my way downstairs. Claire was already up, bless her, making tea. The smell of Earl Grey and toast. Small mercies.

Lily wandered down eventually, still half asleep, dragging her favourite unicorn blanket. Max followed, a whirlwind of energy, already asking for "choc-pops". It's always a mad dash in the mornings, isn’t it? Getting Lily's uniform sorted, finding Max’s missing sock, trying to coax Lily into eating her porridge without a full-scale negotiation. Claire’s a saint, honestly. She manages to stay so calm through it all. I feel myself getting twitchy just trying to make sure Lily's packed lunch is in her bag and Max hasn't smeared jam on his school jumper. Max, for the record, declared his choc-pops "yummy!" and then tried to feed a bit to the cat, Mittens, who was wisely giving him a wide berth.

The drive to QPA Primary with Lily felt quicker than usual. She was telling me about a new book Mrs. Henderson read to them on Friday – something about a brave squirrel. Her imagination is amazing. Just before dropping her off, she looked up and said, "Dad, can we get an ice cream after school today?" I smiled and ruffled her hair, promising we'd see. It’s those little moments, those tiny windows into their world, that make all the stress melt away for a bit. Max’s drop-off at Little Stars was standard – a quick hug, a protest about leaving his toy car, then he was off, already absorbed in the train set. They grow up so fast, don't they? One minute they're newborns, the next Lily's talking about Year 3 curriculum and Max is at nursery.

Commute on the Piccadilly Line was… standard. Hot, crowded, someone’s dubious smelling lunchbox. Used the time to try and clear my head for work, but kept drifting back to the kitchen. Got an email notification from "Bespoke Kitchens London" – another quote in. Opened it quickly, just a glance. Oof. More than I expected. That's for later.

At work, it was straight into the daily stand-up. James was droning on about some backend API issues, Priya was tracking progress on the new client dashboard, and Richard looked like he’d pulled an all-nighter. Senior software engineer life. It’s good, though. Challenging. I like figuring things out, solving problems. And the team’s decent. Got my head down, focusing on refactoring some legacy code for the new payment gateway. It’s satisfying when you finally untangle a particularly gnarly bit of spaghetti code and everything clicks into place. It’s a different kind of problem-solving than trying to figure out if we can afford a Quooker tap or not.

Mid-morning, my lower back started to ache. Knew it. Been meaning to get back to those stretches. Just desk work, constantly. Need to set a reminder to stand up more often. Also, felt my nose tickle – probably the ragweed starting up again. Antihistamines, check. Inhaler, check. My mild asthma usually flares up a bit this time of year too. Just another minor niggle in the grand scheme of things.

Lunch was a sad sandwich from Pret. Used the time to actually dig into that new kitchen quote. Bloody hell. It's a proper bespoke job, definitely at the higher end. Claire's going to freak. Or maybe she won't, maybe she'll love it and then *I'll* freak. We need to sit down tonight and properly compare all three quotes we have now. This is where the spreadsheets come out. My engineer brain loves spreadsheets, but even I’m starting to get dizzy with all the variables. Lead times, fitting costs, appliance packages, materials. It’s like a mini-project management nightmare. But it will be worth it, won’t it? A new kitchen. A proper heart of the home. I try to visualise it, imagine us eating breakfast at the island, Lily doing homework, Max drawing. That helps.

The afternoon at work blurred. More code, some meetings about sprint planning, trying to debug a tricky data pipeline issue. Felt productive, but also drained. The constant low hum of the kitchen reno stress in the background doesn’t help.

Left work a bit late, which meant a mad dash to pick up Max, then Lily. Claire would be home by now, probably marking papers or trying to get a head start on dinner. Got to Max first, he was playing with blocks, proudly showing me a wonky tower. "Daddy!" he beamed. That always brightens things. Picked up Lily, she was chatting excitedly about something that happened in the playground. Her question about ice cream earlier felt miles away.

Got home, dumped bags, kicked off shoes. "Hi, darling," Claire said, already prepping something that smelled delicious – pasta, I think. "Long day?" I nodded, giving her a quick kiss. "You too?" She sighed, "Year 3 drama. Apparently, someone told Mrs. Henderson that Maya cheated on a spelling test." The never-ending cycle of primary school politics.

And then, it happened. I was just about to head into the garden to check on my bike (thinking about a quick spin tomorrow morning if the weather holds) when I heard it. A faint scraping noise. Next door. Dave.

"Oh, for God's sake," I muttered, knowing immediately what it was. Dave has this weird obsession with keeping his side of *our* fence immaculate. Which is fine, except he keeps leaning tools against it, hammering little bits of trellis into it, and generally treating it like it’s his own. And it’s not. It’s ours. We paid for it. This isn't the first time. Or the second. Or the fifth.

I went out, trying to keep my voice even. He was, predictably, trying to screw a bracket into one of the fence posts, presumably to hang a new bird feeder or something equally benign-sounding but utterly destructive.

"Dave," I started, "Mate, what are you doing?"
He looked up, a scowl already forming. He’s about ten years older than me, perpetually grumpy, and seems to enjoy conflict. "Just fixing this. Bird feeder."
"You're screwing into *my* fence post, Dave. We've talked about this. It weakens the post."
"It's barely a screw, Ethan. Just a little one. And it’s *our* shared boundary, isn't it?"
"It's not 'shared' when it's entirely on our property, which it is. And we paid for it. You know that." My voice was rising now. Couldn't help it.
"Oh, here we go," he huffed, rolling his eyes. "Always something, isn't it? What's your problem?"
"My problem, Dave, is you constantly doing things to our property without asking. Last time it was that ivy you tried to train up it, nearly pulled a panel off. Before that, that shed you leaned against it for three months."
"It's a fence! It's there to be used!"
"It's there to mark the boundary and keep our kids safe in the garden, not to be your personal DIY project board! You're damaging it."
"You're making a mountain out of a molehill, as usual, Ethan. Honestly, you're so precious about everything."
That stung. "Precious? I’m just trying to protect what's ours! Would you like it if I started hammering things into your wall?"
"That's completely different!" he spluttered.
"Is it? Is it really? Just please, stop touching the fence. If you want a bird feeder, put a pole in your own garden."
He just glared at me, his face reddening. "Fine," he snapped, yanking the small screw back out, leaving a small, infuriating hole. "But don't come crying to me when it falls down in the next storm."
"It wouldn't be falling down if you didn't keep interfering with it!" I shouted, as he turned his back and stomped off.

I stood there for a moment, seething. My heart was pounding. Bloody Dave. Every single time. It feels so petty, so utterly ridiculous, but it's the principle of it. The constant disrespect for boundaries. It winds me up more than anything. I felt my chest tighten, probably a bit of the asthma flaring from the stress. Took a deep breath.

Went back inside, fuming. "Everything alright?" Claire asked, sensing the tension. I explained, recounting the whole exchange. She just sighed. "Honestly, Ethan. He's not worth it. Just let it go."
"But it's *our* fence, Claire! It costs money to replace those panels!"
"I know, I know. But he's never going to change. Just try to ignore him." She's right, of course. She usually is. But it’s hard to just *ignore* someone actively damaging your property.

The rest of the evening was a bit of a blur of residual annoyance. Dinner was nice, pasta with a rich tomato sauce. Max told me he built the "bestest castle ever" at nursery. Lily showed me a drawing of a rainbow unicorn. Their innocence, their simple joys, were a balm. We did the usual bedtime routine – stories for Max (currently obsessed with dinosaurs), then Lily (a chapter of Harry Potter). Snuggled them both, felt their warmth. It’s amazing how quickly the anger can dissipate when you're holding your children.

After they were finally asleep, Claire and I sat down with the kitchen quotes. The one from Bespoke Kitchens London was indeed eye-watering. Claire agreed. "It's beautiful, but… it's almost an entire year's salary for me, Ethan. Just for the kitchen." She was right. We talked through the pros and cons of all three. The mid-range one from a local company called 'The Kitchen Hub' felt like the sweet spot. Good reviews, good design ideas, and crucially, within a more realistic budget without feeling like too much of a compromise. We agreed to sleep on it, but it felt like a step forward. Decision fatigue is a real thing.

Now, sitting here, journaling, trying to process the day. The argument with Dave still stings a bit. It’s just so unnecessary. But then, if I really think about it, what am I *grateful* for?

1.  **Claire.** Always. Her calm presence, her logic, her patience with me when I get wound up. She's my anchor. Grateful for her and her ability to make a potentially disastrous evening feel normal again.
2.  **The kids.** Lily and Max. Their laughter, their imagination, their hugs. They are the absolute best part of every single day, no matter what crap happens. Max's castle. Lily's brave squirrel. So, so grateful for them.
3.  **My home.** Yes, it needs a new kitchen, and yes, there's a grumpy neighbour and a disputed fence, but it's *our* home. A safe, warm, comfortable space for my family in North London. Mortgage with Barclays feels like a mountain sometimes, but it's ours. And we're making it better, slowly.
4.  **My job.** Even with the long hours and the mental gymnastics, I genuinely enjoy what I do. I work with smart people (James, Priya, Richard included), I'm challenged, and it provides for my family. Grateful for the stability and the intellectual stimulation.
5.  **Small comforts.** That first cup of Earl Grey tea this morning. The satisfying click of code working. The smell of Claire's pasta. The quiet hum of the fish tank in the living room – a little bit of zen in the chaos. The prospect of a bike ride tomorrow morning.
6.  **Perspective on Dave.** Okay, this is harder. But I am grateful that he's just a grumpy neighbour, not something truly awful. And his negativity, in a strange way, makes me appreciate the positive things in my life even more. It's a reminder to actively seek out the good.

It's easy to get caught up in the frustrations – the fence, the kitchen budget, the endless work emails, the back pain. But stepping back, seeing the bigger picture, it's clear I have so much to be thankful for. I think tomorrow I'll make sure to get on the bike early. Maybe take the camera out this weekend. Cook something new. Focus on the good things. Always.

Right. Time for bed. Maybe dream about granite countertops instead of arguments. Max is probably dreaming about dinosaurs. Lily about unicorns. Claire… probably about Mrs. Henderson's Year 3 drama. Good night.
