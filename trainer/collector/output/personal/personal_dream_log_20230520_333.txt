### Dream Log: Ethan Marsh - 2023-05-20

**2023-05-20**

God, where does the time go? Another Saturday, another week gone. Woke up this morning feeling… heavy. Not in a bad way, just a familiar kind of Saturday-morning-exhaustion that hits different after a long week of debugging and endless stand-ups. Max was already up, I think. Heard him humming, or rather, doing his little dinosaur growl from his room around 6:15 AM. Claire groaned, rolled over, pulled the duvet tighter. I just lay there for a bit, staring at the ceiling, tracing the faint crack that needs filling. Add it to the list, I guess. The *other* list, not the kitchen one.

Coffee was the first priority, naturally. My ritual. The smell filling the kitchen is probably the only thing that genuinely pulls me out of that early morning haze. Lily was up by then, at the table, already immersed in her book – 'The Worst Witch' again. She’s devoured that series. Always so quiet when she’s reading, a stark contrast to Max who was by then, predictably, dismantling the Lego tower he’d built last night, brick by chaotic brick, making explosions sounds. "Boom! Crash! Smash!" Yeah, good morning, Max.

While the toast was browning, I quickly checked the fish tank. All seemed well. The neon tetras were schooling tightly, the platies zipping around, looking for food. Even Barry, my grumpy old bristlenose pleco, was out and about, clinging to the glass, polishing it as only he can. It’s a small, consistent bit of calm in the morning chaos. A brief moment of zen before I had to mediate a dispute over who got the last blueberry muffin (Lily won, of course, with her superior logic and heartbreaking pout).

Claire came down, looking a bit more alive after her shower. We exchanged tired smiles. "You heading out with Marcus soon?" she asked, pouring her own coffee. "Yeah, probably around nine. Hampstead Heath loop," I confirmed. She reminded me to take my inhaler before I left, knowing how the pollen count has been. It’s been bothering me more this spring, the usual seasonal allergies exacerbated by the general grime of London. And that persistent lower back twinge I get from too many hours hunched over a keyboard at work. Another thing to add to the 'fix myself' list.

The kitchen. Always the kitchen. It’s looming large, an ever-present hum of anxiety in the background. Yesterday another quote came in, from 'Apex Renovations'. It was… comprehensive. And eye-watering. Claire and I had a brief, slightly tense chat about it last night after the kids were asleep. She’s set on the island, I’m trying to balance that with the sheer cost of moving plumbing and electrics. It’s not just the units, it’s everything *else*. Wiring, plastering, new floor. I briefly scrolled through the spreadsheet I’m building, trying to compare Contractor A's 'basic' quote to Contractor B's 'premium with integrated appliances'. It’s like comparing apples and very expensive oranges. Need to call Marcus later actually, he knows a fair bit about this stuff, did his extension last year.

Right, enough procrastinating. Gear on. Cycling shorts, helmet, water bottle. Quick kiss for Claire and the kids. Max waved enthusiastically, Lily barely looked up from her book. Typical.

The ride with Marcus was exactly what I needed. We met up near Highgate Tube, the sun already warm but with a lovely breeze. Perfect cycling weather. Hampstead Heath felt alive. It always does, but today, with the dappled light through the trees and the faint smell of damp earth and blooming wildflowers, it was particularly good. We did our usual loop – up past Kenwood House, past the ponds where the swimmers were already out, and then the big climb up to Parliament Hill. That’s where the asthma usually kicks in. Took a puff of my Ventolin at the top, just to be safe. The view from Parliament Hill, though, always makes it worth it. You can see the whole city laid out, shimmering in the distance. A little reminder of why we put up with all the chaos here.

We chatted about everything and nothing. Marcus, bless him, is always a good sounding board. He’s in finance too, so we traded war stories about Q1 results and project deadlines. James from my team came up – apparently, he’s having some trouble with the new API integration, and I’m probably going to be dragged into it next week. Just what I need. Marcus also gave me some good tips on kitchen contractors; he used a local guy for his extension who was decent. I made a mental note to ask for the contact details. Then we moved onto kids, schools, mortgages. The usual middle-aged man stuff. We complained about rising costs and the relentless march of time.

And then, it happened. We were cycling down a quieter path, one of those narrower, almost hidden trails near the Vale of Health, when we both simultaneously braked. There it was. A fox. Not a glimpse, not a shadow, but a proper, magnificent fox, sitting calmly by the side of the path, almost regal. Its fur was a rich, fiery red, and its eyes, dark and intelligent, met ours for a long moment. It didn’t seem perturbed at all, just… observing us. We just stood there, silently, watching it. Then, with a flick of its bushy tail, it melted into the undergrowth, gone as quickly as it had appeared.

It was such a small thing, but it just… made the day. A wild, beautiful creature, just existing, oblivious to fintech deadlines or kitchen renovation budgets. It put things into perspective for a moment. Made me feel connected to something bigger, something ancient, right here in the middle of London. I probably sounded like a madman to Marcus, rambling about it for the next ten minutes, but it genuinely lifted my spirits. He just nodded, smiled, said, "Yeah, they’re amazing, aren't they?"

Got home around 1 PM, sweaty but invigorated. Claire was making lunch – tuna melts, a Saturday classic. Lily was drawing a complex scene with fairies and dragons, Max was attempting to climb the bookshelf, thankfully supervised. I jumped in the shower, the hot water a welcome relief on my slightly aching back.

The afternoon was a mix of family time and the persistent pull of the kitchen.
- Lily wanted help with her maths homework – fractions, already! Mrs. Henderson is really pushing them.
- Max insisted on a game of 'chase' which quickly devolved into him just clinging to my leg.
- Managed to sneak in about 45 mins of photo editing – the shots from last weekend’s visit to Kew Gardens. The fox from today would have made an incredible shot, if I’d been quick enough with the camera. Might get the Fuji out tomorrow and head back, see if I can spot it again.
- Made a quick call to Mum in Bristol. She was asking after the kids, and if we were still coming down for half term. George came up, naturally. She misses him a lot, we all do. It's been two years, but sometimes it feels like yesterday. I told her about the kitchen reno, she just sighed, "Oh Ethan, are you sure you need all that fuss?" Always the practical one.
- Text from Hannah – she’s planning a trip down from Manchester next month, which is great. Luke’s still off somewhere in the Highlands, probably composing another obscure folk tune.

Dinner was my turn tonight. Made a lamb tagine, one of my favourites, slowly cooked with apricots and almonds. The kitchen smelled incredible. We ate together, Lily recounting a story about a particularly noisy boy in her class (Harry, I think), Max making airplane noises with his fork. Normal family chaos.

After the kids were finally asleep, stories read, monsters checked for under beds, Claire and I sat on the sofa. Glass of wine for her, cup of tea for me. We went through the Apex Renovations quote again. It’s hard. I want Claire to have the kitchen she dreams of, but the numbers… they’re just so big. We still have the Barclays mortgage, and while we’re doing okay, taking on another huge chunk of debt just for a kitchen feels… overwhelming. We talked about possibly switching the VW Golf for an electric car next year, but that’s another big expense. Maybe push the kitchen a bit? No, Claire reminded me, we agreed to get it done this year. The compromise will be tough.

We talked a bit about work too. Claire’s had a tough week with Year 3 – apparently, a few parents are kicking up a fuss about the school trip dates. My work felt trivial in comparison, pushing pixels and refactoring legacy codebases, even if it is 'fintech'. The constant pressure to innovate, the endless cycle of releases. Priya is great, but Richard can be a real pain with his unrealistic expectations. Sometimes I just want to pack it all in and become a full-time tropical fish breeder. Or a nature photographer. Something that connects me to that fox, not to a server farm.

Ended up reading a bit of a sci-fi novel, but my mind kept drifting. The fox. The kitchen. Tomorrow, I'll dive back into those quotes. Maybe Marcus's guy will be the answer.

**Dream Log Entry:**

Fell asleep thinking about the kitchen, of course.

Dreamt I was cycling through Hampstead Heath, but it wasn't Hampstead Heath. It was a massive, open-plan kitchen. The floor was the grassy path, the trees were giant, gleaming stainless-steel appliances. Marcus was there, but he was wearing a chef’s hat and trying to teach me how to poach an egg on my bike.

Then the fox appeared. But it wasn't a fox. It was a giant, shimmering, translucent spreadsheet, full of numbers and line items, all floating through the air. The fox was chasing it, trying to catch it, but every time it pounced, the numbers would scatter like dandelion seeds. I was trying to shout, "No! Don't let them go! Those are the kitchen quotes!" but no sound came out.

Suddenly, Claire was there, standing on a massive kitchen island that was also Parliament Hill. She was holding a tiny, intricate wrench, looking at me expectantly. "Ethan," she said, her voice echoing across the dream-kitchen-heath, "did you remember to put in the underfloor heating?"

And then everything started to vibrate, and the spreadsheet-fox started howling, and the trees-appliances started to hum a deep, unsettling note. Woke up feeling disoriented, covered in sweat.

Damn kitchen. Even my subconscious can't escape it. Need to remember to check if any of these quotes include underfloor heating. Probably not. And probably another £££.

Tomorrow. Tomorrow is another day of quotes and spreadsheets. And maybe, just maybe, another glimpse of that fox. Or at least, a proper coffee.
