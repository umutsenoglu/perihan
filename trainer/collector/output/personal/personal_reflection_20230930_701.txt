Journal Entry - September 30, 2023

Another Saturday. And it starts exactly how you’d expect a Saturday with Whiskers to start. Woke up to a crash, that sickening tinkle of glass and plastic, followed by a frantic splash. Before my eyes were even properly open, I knew. The fish tank light. Again. Damn cat.

It was 6:15 AM. Claire was still deeply asleep, a peaceful lump next to me, oblivious to the impending aquatic disaster. I swear Whiskers waits for us to be at our most vulnerable, usually pre-coffee or post-midnight. He’d clearly tried to play with the dangling feeding door, misjudged his landing, and knocked the lamp – the LED strip that sits on the rim of the tank – right into the water. Thankfully, it’s low voltage, but still. Electrocution risk, even for the fish. My beautiful neon tetras and corydoras. Poor little guys.

Leapt out of bed, nearly tripped over a discarded dinosaur (Max’s, no doubt). Sprinting down the stairs, heart thumping. Yeah, first thought wasn’t "cat okay?" it was "fish okay? Tank okay?" Pathetic, I know, but those fish are my escape. My tiny, silent world.

Whiskers was sitting on the kitchen counter, licking a paw with an air of complete innocence, as if he hadn't just perpetrated a near-mass electrocution. "Whiskers!" I hissed, pulling the plug for the light with a trembling hand. The tank was fine, no cracks, but the light was floating, half-submerged. The water was a bit cloudy, probably from the shock, and the fish were huddled nervously in a corner. Damn it, Whiskers. It’s the third time this month. We need to secure that light better. Or get rid of the cat. (Joking, mostly).

Spent the next half hour drying everything, checking connections, making sure no water got into the electrics. Managed to get it working again, but it’s definitely on its last legs. Another thing to add to the mental shopping list that seems to grow exponentially these days.

By the time Claire came down, roused by the faint sounds of my grumbling and splashing, I was already on my second cup of coffee, glaring at Whiskers who was now rubbing affectionately against my leg. "Morning, love," she murmured, a soft smile on her face. "Rough start?"
"Whiskers," I grunted, gesturing vaguely at the tank. She just sighed, a sound that conveyed both exasperation and deep, unwavering love for the fluffy terror. "Right. We really need to get those clips sorted. Before he drowns the whole lot."

Lily appeared next, bright-eyed and bushy-tailed, clutching a slightly squashed drawing of Mrs. Henderson. "Daddy, look! It’s Mrs. Henderson and she's got a big red apple!" Max, not far behind, was already pulling at my trousers, demanding toast. The mundane chaos of family life quickly took over, pushing the fish tank drama to the back burner. Breakfast, getting dressed, the usual Saturday morning scramble. Max spilled milk, Lily debated the merits of wearing two different coloured socks, Claire patiently re-braided Lily’s hair for the third time. My lower back was already twinging. Getting old.

Claire was trying to plan the day. "So, the plumber from 'Pipes & Projects' said he could do a video call this morning re: the kitchen layout. And 'Stylish Spaces' sent their revised quote for the cabinets. Did you get a chance to look at it?"
Kitchen renovation. My other ongoing, low-grade stressor. It feels like it’s been months, years even, since we started talking about this. Getting quotes, comparing materials, trying to figure out if we can even afford to do what we *really* want, or if it’s going to be another compromise. This house is great, don't get me wrong. Our 3-bed semi, mortgage with Barclays, in North London. It’s home. But the kitchen… it’s stuck in the late 90s, and it’s too small, too dark. With two growing kids and our love for cooking, it just doesn’t work anymore.

"I'll look at the quote after the plumber call," I promised Claire, trying to sound more confident than I felt. The numbers always make my stomach clench. We’re both on decent salaries – senior engineer, teacher – but London prices for everything are just astronomical. And with two kids, it feels like money just drains out of our accounts. Saving for this kitchen feels like trying to fill a bucket with holes.

After breakfast, Lily settled down to draw more pictures, while Max played with his cars, making realistic (for a 4-year-old) engine noises. Claire disappeared to prep for her Year 3 class next week, probably marking papers or planning some elaborate craft project. I finally sat down with the laptop to tackle the kitchen quotes.

First, the plumber. Gary from 'Pipes & Projects' was surprisingly helpful. Talked through moving the sink, integrating the dishwasher, and where the new radiator could go. The video call was a bit glitchy, but we got there. He seemed competent, which is half the battle. So many cowboy contractors out there.
Then the 'Stylish Spaces' quote. Ugh. £18k for cabinets? That’s just for *cabinets*. Not installation, not appliances, not flooring, not electrics. My head started to spin. Claire wants the shaker style, I want something more modern, but we agreed on a mid-ground. Still, it’s so much. We talked about it last night, about how we'd saved a decent chunk, but the cost of materials has gone up so much. We'd planned for maybe £25k total, but it's looking closer to £40k or even £45k. That's a huge difference.

*   Kitchen to-do:
    *   ✅ Plumber video call (Gary from P&P)
    *   ❌ Review 'Stylish Spaces' revised quote – *ouch*
    *   ✅ Email 'Artisan Build' re: timeline for the extension part (small one, just for extra pantry space)
    *   Call Janet (Mum) later – she’ll want an update.
    *   Figure out electrics for the new light fitting over island.
    *   Think about flooring – LVT vs wood?

Mid-morning, I decided to take Lily and Max to the park. Claire needed a quiet house for a bit, and I needed to clear my head. The bike ride to the park was good, the cool autumn air helping to dissipate some of the financial stress. My asthma usually plays up a bit this time of year with the pollen and changing weather, but today was okay. Cycling is my therapy, the rhythm of the pedals, the wind in my face. Even with the kids in tow, it's good to get out. Max on his balance bike, Lily whizzing ahead on her proper one. They’re growing up so fast.

At the park, watching them play, Lily meticulously drawing in the dirt with a stick, Max shrieking with delight on the swings, I felt a familiar pang of gratitude. Despite the stresses – the kitchen, work, the general grind – these moments are everything. It’s why we do it, isn’t it? For them. To give them a good home, a good life.

Lunch was a chaotic affair of peanut butter sandwiches and apple slices. Max managed to get PB in his hair. Standard.
After naps (for Max, for me, if I’m lucky), I had to pop out. The fish tank light, remember? Decided to try a local aquatic shop rather than waiting for an online order. On the way, I remembered Claire mentioning we needed more milk and some specific gluten-free pasta she uses.

*   Shopping list:
    *   ✅ Fish tank light (definitely a priority)
    *   Milk
    *   GF pasta
    *   Lily's art supplies (she ran out of glitter glue for Mrs. Henderson's portrait)
    *   Maybe a new air stone for the tank, too. Just for good measure.

The aquatic shop was a little oasis. Rows of tanks, the gentle hum of filters, the kaleidoscopic movement of fish. I ended up chatting with the owner for a good twenty minutes about water parameters and filtration. He recommended a slightly different LED strip, one with better clips. £60. Ouch. But necessary. I picked up some new décor too, a little cave for the corydoras. Maybe that’ll distract Whiskers from the light. Wishful thinking.

Back home, I got the new light installed. It looked great, brighter than the old one, and the clips were much more secure. Take that, Whiskers. My aquatic world, safe for now.

The afternoon rolled into evening. Claire was making dinner – her fantastic chicken curry, a favourite. The smell was already wafting through the house. I helped Lily with her Year 3 maths homework – fractions are getting tricky! Max was on the floor, trying to "help" me clean up his toys, which usually involves throwing them back out of the box.

Later, after the kids were bathed and finally tucked into bed, we collapsed on the sofa. Claire had a glass of wine, I had a cup of Earl Grey. "So," she said, "the kitchen quotes. What are we thinking?"
I showed her the 'Stylish Spaces' breakdown. Her eyes widened. "Eighteen thousand? Just for the units? Ethan, that's… that's a lot more than we budgeted for."
"I know," I said, rubbing my temples. My lower back was really aching now, a dull throb. "I think we need to look at 'Kitchen World' again, maybe see if they have a sale on. Or go for slightly less premium finishes."
"But we want it to last, don't we? This is our forever kitchen, hopefully." She looked disheartened. "Maybe we just wait another year? Save more?"
"No, Claire. We can't keep putting it off. The oven's on its last legs, the dishwasher is temperamental, and I'm tired of having zero counter space. We'll make it work. We always do."

We spent the next hour or so just talking, sketching ideas on a notepad. Claire suggested we look at quartz countertops instead of marble, or maybe a different style of tap. Every little saving helps. It's not just the money, it’s the disruption, the dust, the weeks of living out of a temporary kitchen. It feels like a mountain we have to climb.

I checked my phone before bed. A couple of work emails – James asking for a PR review on some payment gateway logic, Priya confirming tomorrow's sprint demo. Richard also sent a meme. Standard Sunday evening dread already kicking in. Tomorrow, back to the fintech grind. Complex distributed systems, microservices, trying to keep ahead of the curve. It's challenging, stimulating, but also draining. Sometimes I feel like an imposter, like I'm just winging it, despite being a 'Senior' engineer. Guess everyone feels that sometimes.

A quick text from Mum, Janet, checking in from Bristol. Just a smiley face. I replied "All good here! Chaos as usual. Call you tomorrow." She worries, especially since Dad (George) passed away in 2021. I need to make more time for her. And Hannah, my sister in Manchester. Luke in Edinburgh. Family.

Before turning in, I checked the fish tank one last time. The new light was glowing softly, illuminating the peaceful underwater world. The tetras were darting, the corydoras sifting the substrate. Whiskers was curled up on the armchair, fast asleep, purring like a tiny engine. He looked so innocent. Such a pain in the arse, but I wouldn't trade him for anything.

Lying in bed, next to Claire, the day's worries and joys slowly settling. The hum of the fridge, the distant sound of traffic from the main road. The kitchen renovation, a huge beast. Work, always demanding. But then the kids' laughter at the park, Claire's smile, the quiet satisfaction of a well-lit fish tank. It’s a lot, this life. But it's good. It’s full. And I wouldn't have it any other way. Time to sleep. Tomorrow is another day. Another sprint. Another step towards a new kitchen. And probably another Whiskers incident.
