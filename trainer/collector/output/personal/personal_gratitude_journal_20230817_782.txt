2023-08-17

Today. Thursday. Another one of those days that felt like it stretched on forever and yet, looking back, just flew by. Hard to believe it’s almost the end of August already. School holidays are definitely a different kind of busy. Claire’s been doing hero work keeping Lily and Max entertained and productive while still doing her own planning for the new school year. Me, I’ve just been trying to keep the fintech engine running.

Woke up before the alarm, as usual. 6:15 AM. That familiar grey light seeping through the curtains. The house was still, save for the hum of the fridge and the gentle gurgle from the fish tank downstairs – always a comforting sound. Lay there for a bit, staring at the ceiling, mentally ticking off the day's to-dos.

*   Project Alpha daily stand-up – need to sync with James on the API integration issue.
*   Priya's chasing for the sprint review demo slides.
*   Portugal flights – seriously need to get those booked. Claire's getting twitchy.
*   Food shop this weekend.
*   New recipe tonight.

That last one had been nudging at me all week. Found a promising-looking creamy tomato and sausage pasta on a cooking blog. Figured it was worth a shot. Usually, anything 'new' with the kids is met with suspicion, if not outright rebellion.

Got up, tiptoed downstairs. Checked on the tropical fish. My rummy-nose tetras are looking particularly vibrant today, really schooling nicely. The water parameters are spot on. It’s a quiet satisfaction, watching them. A little slice of calm before the storm. Had a quick shower, the hot water a blessing on my lower back, which has been playing up a bit this week from all the desk sitting. Must remember to stretch more. The mild asthma usually flares up a bit in the morning chill, too, but today felt clear.

Claire came down around 6:45, already looking more awake than me. She's got that teacher energy, I swear. "Morning, sleepyhead," she’d said, already putting the kettle on. Always grateful for that first cuppa she makes. We chatted quietly about Lily’s swimming lessons and Max’s latest obsession with diggers. The usual stuff.

Then, 7 AM hit, and it was game on. Max, a ball of pure four-year-old energy, was the first to emerge, demanding toast "right now!" Lily, more sedate, drifted down a few minutes later, already clutching one of her books. Mrs. Henderson at QPA Primary has really got her hooked on adventure stories. Breakfast was its usual chaotic symphony of clattering cutlery, spilt milk (Max, obviously), and negotiations over screen time (Lily, trying it on). I tried to mediate while buttering toast and packing Lily’s snack. Feeling that usual morning rush, I found myself grateful for Claire’s calm presence, even in the whirlwind. She’s so good with them.

Drop-offs were smooth enough. Lily skipped into QPA Primary, looking forward to meeting her friends for a playdate at the park nearby after school (a summer club organised by some of the other mums). Max, after an initial wobble, trotted happily into Little Stars Nursery, already eyeing the train set. Gave them both big hugs. Always a pang when they go, but also a quiet relief, knowing they’re happy and well looked after.

My commute into London on the VW Golf was uneventful. Listened to a podcast about sustainable tech, got me thinking again about switching to electric. The 2019 Golf is still running perfectly, but the idea of cutting down on emissions, especially with the kids, is always there. Plus, charging infrastructure is improving. Maybe next year. Pulled into the office car park a bit before 9.

Work was… work. Logged in, grabbed another coffee. James immediately pinged me about the Alpha API issue. Spent a good chunk of the morning debugging it. It was one of those fiddly things, a tiny configuration error that took hours to trace. The satisfaction when I finally found it was immense, a small victory in the grand scheme of things. Priya did indeed chase for the sprint review slides, so I spent my lunch break, scoffing a pre-made salad, trying to cobble something together. Managed to get a quick walk around the block, just to stretch my legs and give my eyes a break from the screen. That back pain was definitely making itself known again.

In the afternoon, I was deep in a new feature development. Richard from across the team popped over to complain about the coffee machine again – some things never change. It was a productive day, though. Got a lot done, felt like I was actually making progress, which isn't always the case. There's a good buzz at the fintech office; it's challenging, but I enjoy the problem-solving aspect of being a senior software engineer.

The drive home. Traffic was a bit heavier than usual. Used the time to call Mum in Bristol. She was asking about the kids, and if we'd booked Portugal yet. "Still working on it, Mum!" I promised. She sounds good, busy with her gardening group. It's good to hear her voice. Sometimes I miss Dad so much, especially when talking to Mum. He'd have loved seeing Lily and Max grow up. Passed away too soon, 2021 was a rough year.

Picked up Lily from the park, Max from nursery. Both were buzzing. Lily was telling me all about a new game they’d invented, and Max had made a very elaborate (and sticky) glitter picture. Their enthusiasm is infectious.

Back home, it was straight into dinner prep. Tonight was the big test: the creamy tomato and sausage pasta. I prepped the ingredients while Lily and Max played in the living room, occasional shrieks of laughter echoing through the house. Claire arrived shortly after, looking tired but happy. She’d had a good day with her Year 3 class planning, apparently. She helped me chop the tomatoes while I browned the sausages and garlic. The kitchen filled with a lovely aroma. I had a moment of quiet gratitude for this kitchen, this home we’ve built. The Barclays mortgage might be a beast, but it’s *our* beast.

The moment of truth: dinner time. Lily eyed it suspiciously. Max, ever the adventurous eater (or just prone to trying anything colourful), took a tentative bite. Then another. Lily watched him. Claire gave me an encouraging smile. "Well?" I asked, trying to sound nonchalant.

Max, mouth full, managed, "Yummy! More, Daddy!"

Lily, after a moment of consideration, picked up her fork. "It's actually... really good, Dad. Can I have some more cheese on it?"

Success! A genuine, unadulterated success. My new pasta recipe was a hit. The kids actually loved it. That, right there, was today's moment. Pure, unadulterated joy in a bowl of pasta. We ate together, talking about their day, my day. Claire reminded me again about the Portugal flights – "Ethan, seriously, before prices go through the roof!" I promised I'd look at them properly after the kids were in bed.

Bedtime routine was surprisingly smooth. Lily read to me from her book, showing off her reading skills. Max requested "just one more story, Daddy, please, please, please!" We ended up doing silly voices for his favourite digger book. Tucked them both in, gave them hugs and kisses. The house fell silent again, a different kind of quiet from the morning. A warm, contented quiet.

Claire and I spent a bit of time on the sofa. We chatted about the Portugal trip, looking at potential dates and flight options on her tablet. Ryanair and EasyJet were showing some decent prices, but they were fluctuating.
*   Check Faro flights for mid-August
*   Look at AirBnBs near Lagos
*   Passport expiry dates (Lily's is due next year, better check Max's too)
*   Ask Hannah if she’s free to mind the fish for a few days (she’s in Manchester, so maybe Luke in Edinburgh is a better bet if he's passing through). Or maybe Janet could come stay.
*   Holiday cover at work – need to tell James/Priya about the dates.

Managed to find a couple of good flight options, pencilled them in. Will book first thing tomorrow. The thought of that holiday, a change of scenery, is a bright spot. We’ve been planning this for months.

Later, I retreated to my study. Didn't feel like cycling tonight, too late, and my back was still a bit stiff. Instead, I sorted through some photos I took last weekend on Hampstead Heath. Love getting out with the camera. There was a particularly good shot of Lily trying to climb a tree. Might print that one. Checked on the fish tank again, just to enjoy the peaceful glow.

Thinking about the day, it wasn’t extraordinary by any means. Just a regular Thursday. But those small moments, they add up. The kids loving the pasta. Claire’s smile. The quiet satisfaction of solving a coding problem. The peace of the fish tank. The promise of a family holiday.

Grateful for:
*   My amazing family – Claire, Lily, Max. Their laughter, even their chaos.
*   That damn pasta recipe actually working. Huge win.
*   A challenging but rewarding day at work.
*   The warmth and comfort of our home.
*   My mum sounding well.
*   The simple joy of a hot shower and a good cup of tea.
*   The little fish bringing a moment of zen.
*   The anticipation of Portugal. Need to book those flights *tomorrow*.

It’s easy to get caught up in the stress – the mortgage, the deadlines, the daily grind. But days like today remind you to look for the good. And sometimes, the good comes in a bowl of creamy tomato pasta, devoured by two very happy kids.

Right, sleep. Tomorrow's another day. More coding. More family time. And definitely, definitely booking those flights.
