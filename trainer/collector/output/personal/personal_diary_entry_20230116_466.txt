Monday. Already. The weekend feels like it was a blur of muddy park runs, attempting to fix that leaky tap in the downstairs loo (failed, again), and just generally chasing our tails. Now it’s Monday morning and the grind is back in full swing.

Woke up before the alarm, which is always a bad sign. 6:15 AM. Just… awake. Brain already spinning up the to-do list for the day, for the week, for life. Max was already stirring, probably heard me. Claire was still out cold, thank god. Let her have another 15 minutes. It’s a battle every morning for her to get out the door for QPA on time.

Coffee. Black. Strong. Stood in front of the fish tank for a few minutes. The Clown Loaches were chasing each other, business as usual. The Neon Tetras glowed, tiny jewels. Always something calming about watching them. It’s a good little escape, even if it’s only for five minutes. Sometimes I wish I could just sit there all morning.

Then the cavalry arrived. Max, bounding in, demanding toast and ‘Paw Patrol’. Lily, dragging herself out of bed, still half asleep, mumbling something about Mrs. Henderson setting them a new project on Ancient Egypt. Already thinking about what photos I have that might fit that theme. Cairo trip from… what was it, 2010? Before kids. Before *everything*.

Breakfast chaos. Toast crumbs everywhere. Max spilling milk. Lily asking if she could wear her wellies to school because of a ‘very important puddle situation’ that only she seems privy to. Claire, bless her, managed to navigate it all with her usual calm efficiency, despite the dark circles under her eyes. She’s been so drained lately, all the winter illnesses doing the rounds at school. Half her class out with flu last week.

Managed to get them both out the door, Lily with her ridiculous sparkly wellies, Max clutching a slightly squashed banana for nursery snack. Waved them off, felt that familiar mix of relief and a pang of guilt that I wasn’t spending more time with them. The London commute calls.

Jumped in the Golf. God, I need to get rid of this car. Every time I get in, I’m just thinking about charging points and range anxiety. But also, the actual anxiety of *selling* it. Checked AutoTrader again at a red light (bad habit, I know). Still the same few VW ID.3s and Kona Electrics around. Nothing really jumping out. The private sale route is such a faff, but the trade-in values they’re offering are just insulting. I paid good money for this Golf, it’s only four years old, low mileage for its age. But the market… sigh.

Priya actually laughed, I just grimaced. My turn: "Yep, on track with the payment gateway integration, just hit a small snag with the API authentication for the German market. Probs have it sorted by lunchtime." Standard.

Got my headphones on, really deep in it. Had to ping Richard from sales about some missing documentation he promised weeks ago. His response, predictably, was "On it, mate! Top priority!" which translates to "I’ll forget about this by lunchtime." Urgh.

We need something practical, something that fits two car seats and a pram (if we ever decide to have a third, god forbid). The ID.3 still feels like the logical step. Familiar VW build quality, but electric. Just need to find one that isn't white.

Afternoon was a blur of code, a quick meeting with my team lead about the sprint velocity (we’re always behind, always), and then trying to help a junior dev with a tricky database query. Felt a dull ache starting in my lower back, probably from hunching over the keyboard for too long. Need to get back on the bike, maybe stretch more. My physio gave me exercises, but when do I ever do them?

Home. The usual dinner rush. Claire got back a little after us, looking utterly wiped. We tag-teamed making pasta and supervising homework. Lily was struggling with her times tables – the 7s are always a killer. Max just wanted to draw on the kitchen table with permanent marker. Managed to avert a disaster.

Finally, kids were bathed and bundled into bed. Lily demanded a chapter of Harry Potter. Max wanted his usual story about the mischievous badger. Read them both, gave them hugs, closed their doors. The house suddenly felt quiet. That familiar quiet, a mix of exhaustion and peace.

Claire collapsed onto the sofa. "God, I'm done. Mrs. Higgins was out today, so I had to cover Year 5 during my PPA time. It was chaos." I rubbed her shoulders. "Sounds rough, love."

Then I remembered. Mum. I'd meant to call her earlier. Checked the time, 8:30 PM. A bit late for Bristol, but she's usually still up. "I'm just going to call Mum," I told Claire. She nodded, already halfway through marking a pile of Year 3 maths tests.

Dialled her number. Rang a few times. "Hello?" Her voice was a little breathy.
"Hi Mum, it's Ethan. How are you?"
"Oh, Ethan, love! I'm alright. Just watching the telly. How are you all?"
We did the usual back and forth. Lily's school, Max's nursery antics, Claire's work, my work. I tried to make it sound positive, not just a list of complaints.

Then I asked how *she* was, really. "You sound a bit tired, Mum."
There was a slight pause. "Oh, just the usual, love. This damp weather always gets to my joints, you know. And the garden… it’s a constant battle, isn’t it? The rose bushes need pruning, and the shed roof is still leaking, I think."
My heart sank a little. The shed roof has been leaking for ages. Dad always used to be on top of that sort of thing. Since he passed, it just feels like the house is slowly falling apart around her, and she’s trying to hold it all together.

"Have you called that handyman I recommended, Mum? For the shed?" I asked, trying to keep my voice even.
"Oh, I keep meaning to. Just so much to do. And money, you know. Everything's so expensive now."
I wanted to offer to just pay for it, but I know she’d refuse. She’s fiercely independent. But she sounds so… weary. Not just physically, but mentally. The loneliness must be immense. It’s been nearly two years now since Dad, but some days it feels like yesterday.

We talked for another half hour. About Hannah in Manchester, Luke in Edinburgh. They both call her too, but it’s not the same as having someone there. I tried to suggest I come down next month, maybe a weekend, help with the garden. "Oh, don't you worry yourself, love. You've got your own family to look after." That familiar line. It’s a shield. She doesn't want to be a burden.

Hung up feeling a knot in my stomach. She *is* tired. More than 'just the usual'. I should call Hannah, see what she thinks. Luke too. We need to figure something out. It feels like such a long way from London to Bristol sometimes.

Back on the sofa with Claire. She looked up from her marking. "Mum alright?"
"She says she is. But… she sounded tired. Really tired. Talking about the garden and the shed again."
Claire sighed. "Oh, Ethan. She just needs help. She can't do it all on her own anymore."
"I know. I just don't know what to *do*. She won't let us help properly."

Claire’s already worried about the extra on the Barclays mortgage for a new car. We need to be sensible. The plan was to use the money from the Golf towards the deposit. But if I can't get a decent price for it…

The practicalities are a headache. But the idea of no more petrol stations, cleaner air for Lily and Max… it's compelling. It's the right thing to do. Just hard to make it happen without feeling like I’m taking out a second mortgage for a car.
