When he finally turned up on my doorstep on March 5th, I was genuinely surprised. Claire opened the door, gave me *that look* – you know, the one that says 'don't screw this up'. He just stood there, looking a bit deflated, holding a cheque. £1,500. Said it was half, and he’d get the rest by April. No fanfare, just a quiet apology about being an idiot. We ended up at The Flask in Highgate later that evening, just like old times. He really opened up then – the divorce had truly messed him up financially, more than I’d realised. Said he was embarrassed, that’s why he’d been so evasive. Hearing that, all my anger just… deflated. I felt like such a prick for the Instagram comment, for assuming the worst.

Things felt better after that. Not totally fixed, but on the mend. We even cycled together a couple of times, out past Highgate Woods. Still, the remaining £1,500 never came in April. Or May. Or June. It’s September now, and he hasn't mentioned it, and neither have I. It’s like this unspoken thing, hanging in the air. When we meet, it’s all surface-level chat – about work (he’s still with the same marketing agency), his new flat, my kids, Lily’s latest Year 3 drama with Mrs. Henderson, Max's nursery antics. We never really get back to the *deep stuff* like we used to. Claire says I should just write it off, forget about it. "Your friendship is worth more than a grand and a half, Ethan," she keeps saying. And she’s probably right. It’s not even about the money anymore, not really. It’s about the trust, I guess. That feeling that he prioritised a new bike over paying me back, even if it was a gift, just… changed something.
