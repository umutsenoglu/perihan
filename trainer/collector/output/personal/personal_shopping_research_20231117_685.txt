Richard keeps pushing for an early deployment, but James is being sensible and pushing back on the testing timeline. Priya’s doing her best to keep us all sane, but the pressure is definitely mounting for this migration. Feels like my brain is just static.

The only break was when I remembered I needed to check my tropical fish – saw the water temp was a degree or two low, quickly adjusted the heater. Small win.

We finally got a chance to sit down after the kids were in bed. Claire poured us both a cuppa, and we opened up the laptop. Those quotes...

The big one: deposit of £5,550 (30%) due by 21st March 2025. That's a chunky amount, but manageable. We’ve been saving, thank God. Still, feels like a lot to commit. Then the rest in two installments.

Claire’s still absolutely fixated on an island. "But Ethan, everyone has an island!" she says. And I get it, I really do. It would make the space so much more functional, for cooking, for the kids to do homework while we're making dinner, for just gathering. But Steve was pretty clear when he was here: the space is just too narrow for a proper island without feeling cramped. It’s a 3-bed semi-detached, not a sprawling mansion.

This is where the debate always grinds to a halt. Claire wants to push for extending into the dining area. It's an option, definitely. We've got the space. But then you’re talking about proper building regs, planning permission, structural engineer, knocking down walls... the cost would balloon, and the disruption would be immense. I mean, we're already talking about essentially living in a building site for a month or more. Adding another few weeks, maybe months, and a huge chunk of cash for an extension? It feels like we're biting off more than we can chew, especially with this work project looming.

I tried to explain my hesitations again tonight. She just sighed. "But it would be *perfect*, Ethan." I know, love, I know. But perfect often means bankrupt. We need to be realistic. We have a Barclays mortgage, and while we're doing okay, we don't have unlimited funds.

I did spend some time looking at appliances on Currys and John Lewis earlier this week, trying to get ahead. Claire wants an induction hob, definitely. And an integrated fridge-freezer – less visual clutter. I’m thinking a decent oven, maybe even a double oven if we can swing it. And a proper quiet dishwasher. Max can make enough noise on his own. Trying to find a good package deal. Everything's so expensive.

I feel this constant pull between the future — our dream kitchen, the kids growing up in a beautiful home — and the present — the crushing weight of work deadlines, the daily grind, the constant juggle. My dad, George, he always had a knack for practical problem-solving. Wish I could talk to him about this. He'd probably tell me to stop faffing and just get on with it, or he'd have some ingenious, cheap solution involving secondhand units and his toolbox. Miss him, especially with these big life decisions.
