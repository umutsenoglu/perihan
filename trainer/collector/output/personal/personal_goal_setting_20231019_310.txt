October 19th, 2023

Okay, another Thursday. Feels like the week just got properly started, and now it’s almost Friday. Which is good, obviously, means the weekend's nearly here. But also, it’s a reminder of how fast time actually flies. Lily was talking about Christmas today, already, after school. She's got that advent calendar excitement bubbling up. Max, bless him, just wants to know if Santa brings diggers. Standard. Claire just gave me *that look* when I mentioned Christmas shopping. "Ethan, it's October. We still haven't booked Portugal." Yeah, I know. It's on the list. Always on the list.

The Portugal trip. God, we need that. A real break. Claire’s been absolutely flat out at QPA Primary. Mrs. Henderson, Lily’s Year 3 teacher, cornered me at drop-off yesterday – something about a parent-teacher meeting next month, reminded me of Claire’s schedule. Claire’s doing so much with those Year 2s, their reading levels, the nativities they’re already half-planning. She’s knackered. Max is loving Little Stars, which is a relief, but the endless stream of sniffles he brings home? Not so much. My turn for the nursery run tomorrow, so I’ll be navigating the morning rush hour traffic through North London, hoping the VW Golf behaves itself. Claire was hinting again about finally looking at electric cars properly. She’s right, probably. That’s a bigger goal for the list.

But today, this afternoon, after staring at lines of code all morning, trying to debug that payment gateway integration James broke (again, seriously, how many times do we have to go over the same API docs?), my mind just kept drifting. Kept going back to cycling. And not in a good way. Not the usual "can't wait to get out this weekend" way. More like… a cold dread.

I think it was the near-miss on the way to work Tuesday. Just a flash, a white van, swerving into my lane without indicating. Nothing actually happened, I braked in time, but it just… shook me a bit more than usual. And ever since then, it’s been nagging at me. Like a low-level hum of anxiety. My mind keeps playing out scenarios. Worst-case scenarios. And for some reason, Marcus, my cycling buddy, has been right at the centre of them.

It’s stupid, really. He’s a good cyclist. Experienced. Careful. But the roads… they’re just getting worse, aren’t they? More impatient drivers, more distractions, bigger vehicles squeezing into gaps that aren’t there. And I keep thinking, what if it was Marcus? What if something really bad happened? I can’t shake this image, this *feeling*, of it actually happening.

I keep picturing it, almost like it’s already happened, which is messed up because it hasn’t. But it’s so vivid. I can almost see him on Highgate Hill, you know, that stretch near the junction where that bakery is? He’d be ahead of me, probably, he’s usually a bit faster on the climbs. We’d be doing our usual Saturday morning route. And then, out of nowhere, this delivery van, a white Transit – I even imagine partial plates, something like KR24 and then just blurred, unreadable letters – just pulls out of a side street. No warning. No looking. Just straight out.

And Marcus, he’d have to swerve. There’d be no choice. Just a split second reaction. And he’d hit the kerb. Hard. And then… I can see it so clearly, him going over the handlebars. That sickening thud. The sound of metal twisting. His Specialized Tarmac, the one he spent four grand on, scraping on the tarmac, probably mangled.

God, it makes my stomach clench just thinking about it. I’d be right there, right behind him. I’d see the whole thing. The panic. That immediate rush of adrenaline mixed with pure fear. My heart would be hammering. I’d be off my bike in a second, rushing to him. Checking if he was conscious. Trying to assess. My hands would be shaking, I know they would. I’d be calling 999, voice probably trembling so much they’d barely understand me. "Highgate Hill. Cycling accident. Unconscious? No, conscious now, but… broken, I think. Collarbone. Ribs." I’d stay with him, of course. Directing traffic. Waiting for the ambulance. It would feel like an eternity.

And then the hospital. Whittington Hospital, probably, it’s the closest. Calling Claire from there. My voice would definitely be trembling then. She’d hear it, she always does. She’d be worried sick. And then going to visit him later that evening, bringing grapes, a cycling magazine. Trying to pretend everything was okay, when inside I’d be a mess. Seeing him there, in a sling, probably doped up on painkillers, maybe a bit dazed. Knowing how long recovery would be. Two broken ribs, a fractured collarbone. That’s not a quick fix. He’d be out of action for weeks, months even.

I even imagine the practical fallout. He’d be discharged a couple of days later, probably Feb 24th, with a whole bag of meds and strict instructions. The police report, ref MET-CAD-2025-2847, the insurance claim against the van driver, all that bureaucratic nightmare he’d have to deal with while recovering. His bike, that beautiful Tarmac, a write-off.

And me… I’d be checking on him, of course. Bringing meals over. Just trying to help. But the whole thing would stick with me. It would change how I cycle. Make me think twice about every junction, every van. Claire would be even more worried than usual. "Can’t you just cycle less on main roads, Ethan?" she’d say. And she’d be right to. I’d probably end up ordering a new helmet straight away, something even safer, like that Giro Aether I’ve been looking at, £220, better front lights too. Just anything to feel a bit more protected. And I know the cycling club would do a whip-round, they always do for stuff like this. Probably £350 for physio, maybe more.

It’s just… it’s been playing on my mind all day. The fragility of it all. How quickly things can change. And it makes you think, doesn't it? About what really matters. About what you’re doing with your time. What you want to achieve. What are the *real* goals? Beyond just getting through the day, beyond just hitting deadlines at work or getting the kids fed and bathed.

It’s October 2023. Nearly the end of the year. Time to actually sit down and put some structure to this swirling mess of thoughts. I need to make some tangible goals. Things to work towards. Not just for me, but for us. For Claire, for Lily, for Max. For Mum, for Hannah, for Luke.

Right. Let’s get this down.
