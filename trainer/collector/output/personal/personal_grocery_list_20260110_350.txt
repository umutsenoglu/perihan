**January 10th, 2026**

Friday. Thank god. This week has been... a lot. Not even sure where to begin. Just sat down with a cuppa, Claire's already asleep, kids have been out cold for hours. Should really tidy up the kitchen but my back is aching, and honestly, my brain feels like scrambled eggs.

Woke up at 6 again. Max had been restless, pretty sure he kicked me in the face around 4. Lily was dreaming loudly about unicorns or something, so no actual quiet time before the alarm. Claire was already up, always is, getting her bits ready for school. We did the usual morning dance – cereal for Lily (she insisted on the sugary stuff, "just this once, Daddy!"), porridge for Max (half on the floor, half in his mouth, the usual), school bags, packed lunches. Claire made a mental note to check if Lily needed new wellies after she mentioned a puddle jumping incident yesterday. QPA Primary, bless them, always sending notes about appropriate outdoor gear. Max was excited about "story time with Mr. Ben" at Little Stars. Sometimes I miss those simpler days, you know? Just pure, unadulterated excitement over a picture book.

Managed to grab a quick coffee before heading out. The commute was a nightmare, as expected on a Friday. Some idiot in a van nearly clipped my Golf on the A406. Really need to get that EV sorted. Claire's been nagging me about it – "Think of the planet, Ethan, and the ULEZ charges!" She's right, obviously. Just another thing on the never-ending list.

Got into work, straight into the stand-up. James was in a mood, something about the new payments module being behind. Priya, bless her, looked exhausted, probably dealing with a toddler herself. Richard just nodded, as usual, probably thinking about his next ultra-marathon. Spent most of the morning trying to debug that recurring API issue for Project Chimera. The one where the latency spikes randomly, throwing off the reconciliation process. It's driving me mad. Feels like I've stared at the same lines of code for days.

Then, the email. Subject: "Freelance Project - API Integration for 'NextGen Health'". Opened it, half-expecting another spam message. But it was from Sarah from the old network, the one I met at that FinTech London event last year. Remembered her – sharp, no-nonsense. The offer itself looked good. Really good. Double my hourly rate at work, minimum 20 hours a week for 3 months. Remote, flexible hours.

My first thought: *Wow. That could sort out the boiler fund, or finally get us that EV without dipping into savings.* My second thought, almost immediate: *Where the hell am I going to find 20 hours a week?*

Between work, the kids, trying to keep the house from falling apart, and now... Mum. That's the real kicker, isn't it?

Called Mum at lunch, as usual. She sounded a bit more tired today. Said her knee was "playing up something rotten." She's trying to be brave, I know, trying to keep up the façade for me. But I hear it in her voice. The slight tremor, the pauses. The way she tries to change the subject when I ask about the carers. She mentioned the district nurse had been by, changed her dressing, but she was worried about "bothering" them again. Guilt, that familiar knot in my stomach. She's in Bristol, and I'm here, staring at a screen, troubleshooting someone else's broken code.

Claire and I talked last night about visiting. We tentatively pencilled in the first weekend of Feb. It's Lily's half-term, so we could make a proper trip of it, maybe stay a few days. But that means pulling Max out of nursery for a couple of days, and Claire losing precious weekend time she needs to mark books. And the drive... God, the M4. Plus, I need to coordinate with Hannah and Luke. Hannah's juggling her own three kids and a demanding job in Manchester, Luke's busy up in Edinburgh, "important research" he calls it. We need a family meeting, virtually at least, to properly figure out the care rota. It's not fair on Mum, or on any of us, winging it. Dad (George) would have known what to do. Always had a plan. Miss him like mad, especially now.

The freelance project. Logged out of work a bit early, 5:30. Skipped the gym again. Back's been iffy all week, probably from slouching over the laptop at work. Should really go for a cycle tomorrow, clear my head. Or maybe just potter around the fish tank. The dwarf gouramis have been looking a bit lethargic lately, need to check the water parameters again. Might need a partial water change. It’s my little slice of calm, that tank. Like watching a tiny, silent, underwater soap opera.

Got home, Claire was already wrestling with dinner. "Lily wants spaghetti Bolognese again, Max insists on fish fingers. Compromise?" I just nodded, grateful. The usual chaos ensued. Lily telling me all about her classmate Tom drawing a rude picture of Mrs. Henderson (I stifled a laugh, poor Mrs. Henderson), Max trying to feed his broccoli to the dog (we don't have a dog, it was a toy rhino). Bath time, stories. Max, "Daddy, read *The Gruffalo* again!" Lily, "No, *Matilda*! Chapter 3!" Negotiating peace treaties between them is harder than any sprint planning meeting.

After they were finally down, Lily's soft breathing, Max still occasionally muttering about the Gruffalo in his sleep, Claire and I sat down. Shared a glass of wine. I brought up the freelance offer.
Her reaction was... predictable. A mix of excitement about the money and immediate concern about the time. "Ethan, darling, where? You're already working 9-6 most days, then helping with the kids. When would you even do it? Midnight oil again? You were a wreck last time you did that." She's right. I know she is. The last freelance gig nearly killed me, remember falling asleep at my desk more than once. But the money... it could make such a difference right now, with Mum's situation looming, and general cost of living. We talked it through, round and round. No easy answer. I told her I'd draft a polite reply to Sarah, saying I'd consider it over the weekend, see if I could scope it down or find a slot. Maybe a couple of hours in the mornings before work, and then the weekends? But then what about family time? What about *my* time? Photography has been gathering dust, haven't touched the bike in weeks. My lower back twinges just thinking about it.

Claire then reminded me about the grocery list for tomorrow. "We're out of milk, and Max's favourite yoghurt, and I promised Lily pancakes for breakfast." So I scribbled a few things down on the back of an old envelope:

- [ ] Milk (full fat for kids)
- [ ] Bread (sourdough if they have it, for me)
- [ ] Eggs (free range)
- [ ] Max's yoghurts (strawberry only)
- [ ] Flour (plain) & baking powder (for Lily's pancakes)
- [ ] Bananas (Max eats like a monkey)
- [ ] Apples (for Lily's school snack)
- [ ] Spinach (for Claire's green smoothies, bless her)
- [ ] Coffee beans (running low, emergency!)
- [ ] Some sort of pasta sauce (quick dinner option next week)
- [ ] Fish food (tropical flakes) – *add to note for tank*
- [ ] Paracetamol (for my back, and maybe Mum if I see her soon)
- [ ] Toilet paper (essential!)

That's the basic list. But it's more than just groceries, isn't it? It's the daily fabric, the small things we do to keep everything ticking over. And into that fabric, all these other threads get woven – work pressures, Mum's health, financial decisions, the kids growing up too fast.

Sometimes I just want to pause it all. Just one quiet, uninterrupted moment to think. Maybe the bike ride tomorrow will help. If I can fit it in. If the weather holds. And if Max doesn't decide 5 AM is the perfect time to start demanding his strawberry yoghurts.

The freelance project... it's a blessing and a curse. More work, more money, less time. The perpetual dilemma. And Mum... she needs us. I need to make sure she knows that.

Right. Bed. Tomorrow's another day. Probably starting at 6 AM again.
G'night. Ethan.
