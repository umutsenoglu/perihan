January 29th, 2024
Monday. Another week. Bleary-eyed start, as usual. Max was up at 5:45 wanting a "special train" on YouTube, which meant the iPad and a very short leash from Claire. Lily actually slept in a bit, which felt like a minor victory. Grabbed a quick coffee, instant, not even time for the Nespresso pod. Claire was already in the kitchen, packing lunchboxes, a whirlwind of efficiency. How she does it, I’ll never know. She mentioned something about Year 3 needing extra craft supplies this week – "Mrs. Henderson wants glitter, Ethan, *glitter*." My internal groan was silent, thankfully.

Out the door by 7:15. Commute felt longer than usual. The Northern Line was its usual sardine can self, someone’s elbow perpetually in my ribcage. Tried to catch up on some industry news, but my mind kept drifting. The inheritance thing again. Still no firm timeline from Davies & Sons on George’s estate. It just hangs there, a low hum of unresolved business. Mum calls every other day asking for an update, bless her. She’s trying to be stoic, but I can hear the weariness in her voice. Losing Dad... it changed everything. Not just emotionally, but all these bloody practicalities. Paperwork, solicitors, bank statements from God knows when. Claire’s been amazing, keeping me grounded, but it’s a constant weight.

Got into the office just before 8:30. James was already at his desk, staring intently at code, probably trying to debug whatever Priya broke late last week. Fintech never sleeps, or at least our little corner of it doesn’t. My sprint review for the new API integration is looming. Richard wants tighter security protocols, which means more refactoring than I initially budgeted for. Spent the first hour wrestling with a particularly stubborn database query. My lower back was already complaining by 10 AM. Desk work, eh? The curse of the modern age. Really need to get back to those stretches. Or just buy a standing desk. Claire suggested it for Christmas but I laughed it off. Not laughing now.

During my lunch break, a sad desk salad, I found myself making a mental list for next year. Luke’s birthday. April 18th, 2025. Yeah, it’s over a year away, but Mum’s already mentioned it a couple of times. "I really must go up and see Luke for his birthday next year, Ethan. You'll have to sort out my flights for me, dear." That familiar request. The little knot in my stomach tightened. I love Mum, truly, but booking travel for her is an exercise in patience that I rarely possess.

It’s not just her general technophobia, it’s the indecisiveness. The back-and-forth is inevitable. I can already hear it:
"Oh, are you sure those dates are right, Ethan? Luke said come earlier."
"But I have that doctor's appointment on the Monday, don't I? Maybe shorter."
Then the airline choices. "Ryanair looks cheaper, doesn't it?" she’ll say, even if I’ve just spent an hour explaining the hidden fees or the horrific connection through some godforsaken airport she’d have to navigate on her own. I remember trying to get her to use a self-service check-in once. She ended up asking a baggage handler for help with her "phone ticket" for twenty minutes. Good God. It’s always me, the tech support son. Hannah's too busy with her own kids up in Manchester, and Luke... well, Luke is Luke. He'd probably just send her a link to a booking site and be done with it.

Back to work. Spent the afternoon in a code review with Richard. He’s sharp, but his attention to detail can be exhausting. Every comma, every indentation. I appreciate it, honestly, but sometimes I just want to ship the damn feature. My asthma felt a bit twitchy today too, probably the cold air mixed with the recycled office air. Or maybe just stress.

Home by 6 PM. Collected Max from Little Stars Nursery – he was covered in paint, naturally, but happy. Then QPA Primary for Lily. She was buzzing about a story she wrote in Mrs. Henderson's class about a magical unicorn that grants wishes. I smiled and nodded, mentally noting to ask Claire about it later. The walk home was chilly but clear.

Dinner was one of my go-to recipes: roasted chicken with new potatoes and asparagus. Comfort food. Claire arrived just as I was plating up, looking knackered. Teaching primary school kids all day takes a special kind of energy. We chatted about our days. Lily regaled us with unicorn tales while Max tried to feed his chicken to his toy dinosaur. Normal chaos.

After the kids were finally asleep, around 8:30, and the kitchen was mostly tidied, Claire and I collapsed on the sofa.
"Heard from Mum today," I said, rubbing my temples.
Claire sighed gently. "Everything okay with the solicitor?"
"Same old. Just… waiting. She's getting fed up, I think. And the house in Bristol still feels like Dad's, you know? Like we're intruding trying to sort it out."
She nodded, reached for my hand. "It's a lot, Ethan. For all of you."
"Yeah. And she's already dropping hints about needing flights for Luke's birthday next year." I told her.
Claire chuckled, a low, knowing sound. "Oh no. Not again. The 'Ryanair looks cheaper' saga mark two?"
"Precisely," I sighed. "And the 'I don't trust phone tickets' lecture, and the 'can I bring a suitcase AND a handbag' debate. I swear, the woman thinks baggage policies are written specifically to annoy her."
Claire laughed properly then. "She just wants to see her boys. And you're the chosen one, the tech wizard."
"The reluctant tech wizard, more like."

It’s true though. I know it’s coming. The constant phone calls, the screenshots I'll have to send, the repeated explanations of routes and times. She’ll complain about the neighbour’s dog while I’m trying to confirm her return date. She’ll tell me what she had for dinner yesterday just as I'm about to click 'confirm booking'. She’ll send blurry photos of her garden, asking me if the hydrangeas are supposed to be that colour. And then, once it's booked, the inevitable secondary questions about hand luggage and boarding passes. I’ll end up printing them and posting them, probably. It's just... Mum.

The inheritance thing is so much heavier though. Dad would have had it all sorted, meticulously. He was always so organised. It feels like we're constantly trying to catch up to his foresight, but without him here to guide us. The thought of sorting through his study, all his old things… it still feels too raw. Mum's been putting it off, and I don't blame her. It's more than just a house; it's a lifetime of memories. And the solicitor’s fees keep mounting. Just another thing to worry about.

Decided to give the tropical fish tank a quick clean. There’s something meditative about watching the colours shift in the water, the quiet hum of the filter. It's a small pocket of calm in a generally frenetic life. The Kuhli loaches were darting around, happy. Managed to clear my head a bit.

Checked my old VW Golf in the driveway. Still running well, but the thought of switching to electric keeps nudging me. Financial incentives, environmental impact… Claire’s keen. But another big expense. Barclays mortgage already takes a decent chunk each month. One thing at a time.

Poured myself a glass of water, took my inhaler – just a routine puff, nothing major, but a reminder. Back feels a bit better after some stretches I did while watching the fish.

Thinking about tomorrow. Another day of sprint work, another layer of emails from Davies & Sons. And then the quiet dread of next year’s "Help me book flights, Ethan" request, already taking root in the back of my mind. Just another thing on the infinite to-do list of life, parenting, and being the responsible son. Goodnight, journal. Need to sleep.
