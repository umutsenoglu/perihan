**2025-04-04 - Friday**

Right, Friday. End of the week, thank god. Been a pretty mad one. First thing though, gotta deal with this bloody algae. Woke up this morning and the Anubias in the main tank looked like it had grown a beard of green fuzz overnight. Not good. I knew it was coming, noticed a slight greenish tint to the water yesterday but thought maybe it was just the light. Nope. Full-blown outbreak.

Checked the lighting timer this morning – still on 8 hours. Maybe I need to drop it to 6 for a bit. Water change yesterday wasn't enough clearly. I should have done a proper parameter check then. Rookie error. Grabbed the API Master Test Kit before I properly woke up.

*   pH: 7.4 (stable, good)
*   Ammonia: 0 ppm (as expected, tank's well-cycled)
*   Nitrite: 0 ppm (same)
*   Nitrate: *Whoa*. 40 ppm. That's definitely higher than I'd like. Been slipping on the water changes, haven't I? Max was sick last week, then Lily had that school project, and work's been mental with the Kestrel project deadline looming. No excuses though, my poor Neons.

So, high nitrates. Classic algae fuel. Explains it. Also, probably slightly overfeeding the little guys. Max tends to get a bit enthusiastic with the flakes when he "helps" me. Need to get a handle on that. Another 30% water change is on the cards for this evening. And probably again on Sunday. Less food, more frequent small water changes. Maybe add a few more fast-growing plants like Cabomba to outcompete the algae for nutrients, or a couple of Amano shrimp. Always wanted Amanos. Might be the excuse I need. The Otos are doing their best but they can only do so much. The bristlenose pleco just kinda hangs out on the bogwood, looking zen. Wish I could be that chill.

Work today was… fine. Another retrospective on Kestrel Sprint 3. James was trying to be upbeat, but you could tell everyone's knackered. Priya looked like she'd been up all night coding – probably was. My module for the real-time transaction processing pipeline seems solid, passed all the integration tests, but I’m still worried about the edge cases. Always the edge cases. Richard from the compliance team had a minor meltdown about some logging requirements, but we talked him down. Just standard fintech stuff, layers upon layers of bureaucracy on top of complex code. At least I was WFH today. Didn't have to face the Northern line at rush hour. Still, the back's a bit stiff from sitting at the desk. Need to remember to stand more often, maybe use the standing desk attachment Claire got me for my birthday. It's just... it's rarely convenient when I'm deep in thought or a meeting.

Lunch was leftover pasta. Nothing inspiring. Then a quick cycle around the block to clear my head before the afternoon meetings. Felt good, even if it was just 15 minutes. London roads are always a bit insane, but the quiet residential streets near us are decent.

Picked up Lily from QPA. She was buzzing about something Mrs. Henderson said about "star writers." Max was at Little Stars Nursery, drawing aliens again. "You know," she said, leaning against the counter, watching me almost chop my finger off, "Sarah from school just got a puppy. Golden Retriever. Absolute fluffball. Lily would love one, Ethan."

"A fluffball that needs training, walks in the rain, vets bills, and will probably eat my bike tires," I countered, perhaps a little unfairly. I'm not *against* the idea, not really. It's just the logistics of it. We're both working, the kids are still little, the house isn't huge.
"It'd be amazing for them, though. Imagine Lily taking it for walks in the park. Teach them responsibility."
"And who'll be doing those walks at 6 am on a freezing January morning?"
She gave me *the look*. "We'd share. And Labradors are so good with kids. You know how much I've always wanted a Lab."
Yeah, I know. Since before we even met, probably. She grew up with a Collie, I think. I had a goldfish when I was a kid. Different scales, literally.
"I've been looking at some local breeders," she continued, undeterred. "And rescue centres. There's a lovely Labrador cross at the Battersea Dogs & Cats Home website. He's called 'Barnaby.'"
Barnaby. Already got a name. This is serious.
"Okay, okay," I said, putting down the knife. "Let's talk properly about it over the weekend. What's the realistic time commitment? The cost? What about when we go on holiday? Who looks after it? What about the algae in my fish tank, Claire? I have a crisis right here."
She just laughed. "Priorities, Ethan. Prior-it-ies."
She's right, partly. It's a huge decision. My mum, Janet, would probably love having a dog around when she visits from Bristol, but she's not here often enough to be a daily help. Hannah in Manchester has her two cats, definitely not a dog person. Luke in Edinburgh... well, Luke would probably offer to "dog-sit" then hand it off to his girlfriend.

Dinner was good though. Lily helped me stir the chilli. Max mostly just played with his spoon.
Later, after Lily was in bed (reading *Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets* again, she's obsessed) and Max was finally asleep after fighting it for an hour, I did the water change. Dosed with a bit of flourish Excel to try and nuke the algae. Fingers crossed. Also spent some time scrolling through Labrador pictures online. They *are* cute. Big, floppy ears. Those earnest eyes. Oh god, I'm weakening, aren't I?
Need to make a list of pros and cons. A proper, engineer's list.
