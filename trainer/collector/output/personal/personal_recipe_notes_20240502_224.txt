**Slow-Cooked Beef & Ale Stew - Proper Comfort Food**

**Date:** 2024-05-02

**Ingredients:**
*   ~1kg Beef Shin or Chuck (got a decent piece from Lidgate's this morning, bit pricey but worth it)
*   2 large onions, roughly chopped
*   3-4 carrots, chunky
*   2 celery stalks, chopped
*   2 cloves garlic, crushed (or 3, for Max)
*   1 tbsp plain flour
*   500ml good quality dark ale (picked up that local craft ale from the Co-op, 'North London Brew Co. Dark Horse Stout')
*   500ml beef stock (might need a bit more if it reduces too much)
*   
*   1 tbsp tomato paste
*   1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce (for that umami kick, Claire loves it)
*   2-3 bay leaves
*   Sprig of fresh thyme (or 1 tsp dried, if I can't find fresh)
*   Salt & pepper to taste
*   A little oil (olive or veg) for browning
*   Maybe some button mushrooms later? Lily probably won't eat them but Max sometimes does.

**Instructions:**
1.  **Prep:** Chop everything. Trim any excess fat from the beef (though shin needs a bit for flavour). Pat beef dry.
2.  **Sear:** Heat oil in a large oven-safe pot/Dutch oven (my old Le Creuset will do the job, though thinking about a new, bigger one for the new kitchen... if we ever actually agree on the layout). Brown beef in batches until nicely coloured. Don't overcrowd the pan. Remove beef and set aside.
3.  **Sauté:** Add onions, carrots, celery to the pot. Cook until softened, ~8-10 mins. Add garlic for the last minute. This is the bit that fills the kitchen with smell, always makes the house feel homely. Pity our kitchen is so cramped for two people, makes this whole cooking thing more of a ballet than a pleasure
4.  **Flour & Liquids:** Stir in the flour and cook for a minute or two, letting it coat the veggies. Then tomato paste and Worcestershire sauce – proper depth of flavour. Pour in the ale, scraping up any brown bits from the bottom of the pot. Bring to a simmer, let it bubble for a minute to cook off some of the alcohol. Add the beef stock, bay leaves, thyme, and return the browned beef to the pot. Make sure the beef is mostly submerged.
5.  **Simmer & Season:** Bring it all back to a gentle simmer. Taste and season generously with salt and pepper. This is crucial for slow cooking, needs a good base.
6.  **Slow Cook:** Cover the pot tightly (aluminium foil under the lid helps seal it) and transfer to the preheated oven at 150°C (Gas Mark 2). Cook for at least 3 hours, maybe 3.5-4 hours until the beef is meltingly tender. Check it occasionally, stir, and add a splash more stock if it looks too dry.
