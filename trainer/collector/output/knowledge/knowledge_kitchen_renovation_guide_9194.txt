**Ethan Marsh - Kitchen Renovation Project Notes**

**Project Title:** Kitchen Remodel – North London Semi
**Date:** 2024-03-12 (Tuesday evening, post-kids-bedtime)
**Primary Driver:** Claire (and my sanity while cooking). Current kitchen is functionally poor and aesthetically dated.

---

**1. Initial Thoughts & Goals**

*   **Why now?** The kitchen is almost 20 years old, inherited from previous owners. It's cramped, dark, and the appliances are starting to fail. We've been talking about this for years, but with Lily and Max getting older, the current layout just isn't working for family life.
*   **Core Problem:** Lack of space, poor workflow, limited storage, no proper dining area. Claire hates the yellow tiles (fair point, they're truly awful). I hate trying to cook a proper meal without enough counter space.
*   **Desired Outcome:**
    *   **Open-plan:** Integrate the current kitchen with the separate dining room. This is non-negotiable for Claire. Create a light, airy space.
    *   **More functional:** Better workflow, ample counter space (island?), efficient storage.
    *   **Family-friendly:** Durable, easy to clean, safe for Max's inevitable chaos. A spot where Lily can do homework or draw while we cook.
    *   **Modern aesthetic:** Cleaner lines, updated look.
    *   **Better garden access:** Current single door is restrictive. Bifold or sliding doors?
    *   **Improved cooking experience:** For me, this is key. Good hob, decent oven setup.

---

**2. Budget & Financing**

*   **Claire's initial estimate:** £25,000 - £30,000. *My gut feeling is that this is optimistic for North London, especially with knocking down a wall and potential extension work.*
*   **Our Savings:** We have about £15k in the ISA. Not enough.
*   **Mortgage Further Advance:** Our current mortgage is with Barclays. *Need to investigate their rates and how much equity we have available.* This is likely the most cost-effective option for a larger sum.
*   **Personal Loan:** Less desirable, rates usually higher.
*   **Contingency:** Absolutely essential. At least 15%, maybe 20% given the structural changes. So, if we aim for £30k, budget for £34.5k - £36k.
*   **Lily's Tutoring:** This is an unexpected (but necessary) expense. Mrs. Henderson flagged her struggles with fractions. Tutor will be £40-£50/hour. This *will* impact the kitchen budget – either we scale back slightly, or push the overall budget higher. *Need to have a serious conversation with Claire about the final budget ceiling, acknowledging the tutoring cost.*

**3. Design & Layout Considerations**

*   **Current Layout Issues:**
    *   Narrow galley kitchen, feels like a corridor.
    *   Fridge-freezer (our current 2007 model) sticks out, blocking light and flow.
    *   Dishwasher is a relic, noisy, and inefficient.
    *   Dining room is separate and underutilised.
*   **Desired Features:**
    *   **Wall Removal:** Between kitchen and dining room. *Crucial first step: get a structural engineer in. Need to check if this is Permitted Development or requires planning permission (unlikely for internal, but good to check).*
    *   **Island/Peninsula:** For prep space, informal dining, and social hub. Claire dreams of this. Maybe with a small overhang for stools.
    *   **Pantry/Larder Cupboard:** Dedicated food storage. Pull-out mechanisms look good. Tired of shelves being a chaotic mess.
    *   **Integrated Appliances:** For a sleek, uncluttered look.
    *   **Bi-fold/Sliding Doors to Garden:** Replacing the single back door. This will bring in so much light. *Potentially a big cost item, but could transform the back of the house.*
    *   **Kids' Zone:** A comfortable spot at the island or a small banquette seating area where Lily can do homework or Max can draw while we're in the kitchen.
    *   **Tropical Fish Tank:** Our 120L tank is currently in the living room. If we're opening up the space, could it be integrated into the new kitchen/dining area? *Requires stable temperature, away from direct sunlight/hob heat. This would be a nice focal point.*

**4. Materials & Finishes**

*   **Worktops:**
    *   **Quartz:** Claire's preference. Durable, non-porous, wide range of colours/patterns (she likes the marble-effect look). *Expensive, but might be worth it for longevity and ease of cleaning.*
    *   **Granite:** Also durable, natural. *Heavy, needs sealing, potentially higher cost.*
    *   **Laminate:** Cheaper, but not the long-term quality we want.
    *   *Need to get samples. Test for stain resistance. Consider a lighter colour to maximise light.*
*   **Cabinetry:**
    *   **Style:** Claire likes handleless or minimalist slab doors for a modern look. I'm leaning towards a modern shaker style – a bit more timeless, maybe less prone to showing fingerprints than true handleless.
    *   **Colour:** Claire's keen on a dark green or deep blue for the base units, with lighter (off-white/light grey) wall units. I actually quite like the dark green idea.
    *   **Material:** MDF with a good quality paint finish is common. *Durability with kids is paramount.*
    *   **Internal Storage:** Pull-out drawers, magic corners, integrated spice racks. These add cost but massively improve usability.
*   **Flooring:**
    *   **LVT (Luxury Vinyl Tile):** Strong contender. Durable, warm underfoot, water-resistant (essential with Max!). Can mimic wood or stone.
    *   **Large Format Tiles:** Hard-wearing, easy to clean. *Can be cold, and grout lines can get dirty quickly. Would need a good quality grout.*
    *   **Engineered Wood:** Looks great, but not ideal for high-spill areas, especially with Max.
    *   *Need something that won't show every crumb or muddy footprint from the garden.*
*   **Splashback:**
    *   **Tiled:** Metro tiles, perhaps herringbone pattern (Claire's latest Pinterest obsession).
    *   **Glass:** Clean, modern, easy to wipe down.
    *   **Upstand:** Minimalist, but less protection.
    *   *Behind the hob needs to be particularly robust to handle my cooking antics.*

---

**5. Appliances**

*   **Hob:** Induction. Non-negotiable. Faster, more energy-efficient, safer with kids (cools quickly), and so much easier to clean. Claire agrees on this.
*   **Oven:** Double oven, or a single oven plus a combi-microwave/oven. *Essential for Sunday roasts and when entertaining Mum Janet when she visits from Bristol.* Self-cleaning function is a big plus.
*   **Fridge-Freezer:** Integrated. Either a large single integrated or integrated side-by-side. *Must be frost-free.*
*   **Dishwasher:** Fully integrated, and critically, *quiet*. Our current one sounds like a small aircraft.
*   **Extractor Fan:** Powerful and quiet. *Needs to be externally vented for my asthma, not recirculating.*
*   **Microwave:** Integrated, either part of a combi-oven or a standalone unit.
*   **Hot Water Tap:** Quooker or Grohe Red. Claire wants one, and I'm quite tempted for instant tea/coffee and cooking. Saves kettle clutter.
*   **Brands:** Bosch, Siemens, Neff seem to be reliable. *Consult Which? reviews extensively.*
*   **Energy Efficiency:** A key consideration. We're already looking at switching the VW Golf to electric, so every bit of energy saving in the house helps.

---

**6. Lighting & Electrics**

*   **Lighting:**
    *   **Ambient:** Recessed spotlights throughout, on dimmers.
    *   **Task:** Under-cabinet LED strips for prep areas.
    *   **Accent:** Pendant lights over the island. Claire has some specific ideas for these.
    *   **Natural:** Maximise with the new doors and potentially a larger window.
*   **Electrics:**
    *   **Sockets:** Plenty! More than we think we need. Especially around prep areas, by the island (for laptops/phones), and dedicated sockets for small appliances (kettle, toaster, coffee machine).
    *   **USB charging points:** Integrated into some wall sockets would be very useful.
    *   **Smart Home Prep:** Wiring for smart speakers, maybe a wall-mounted tablet for recipes/music.

**7. Contractors & Project Management**

*   **Option 1: Full-service Kitchen Company (e.g., Magnet, Howdens, Wren, local independent).**
    *   Pros: One point of contact, design service, supply, installation. Potentially less stress.
    *   Cons: Less flexibility on materials, potentially higher cost, can be sales-driven. *Claire prefers this idea for ease.*
*   **Option 2: Independent Designer + Separate Trades.**
    *   Pros: More bespoke, independent advice, potential for better value.
    *   Cons: Requires significant project management from us. *My job is demanding; fitting this in would be tough. Mum Janet always managed her own trades, said it saved money but was a headache.*
*   **Quotes:** Get a minimum of three detailed quotes for comparison. *Absolutely essential.*
*   **References & Insurance:** Crucial. Check online reviews (Google, Nextdoor).
*   **Contracts:** Clear scope of work, payment schedule (milestones, not just upfront cash), and realistic timelines.
*   **Timeline:**
    *   Design Phase: 1-2 months.
    *   Ordering/Delivery: 6-12 weeks for cabinetry, bespoke items.
    *   Installation: 3-4 weeks if structural work is involved. Could be longer.
    *   *Total project duration: Minimum 4-6 months from design approval to completion. Need to manage Lily and Max's expectations for disruption.*
*   **Cooking During Renovation:** Microwave in the living room, BBQ (weather permitting), takeaways. *Max mainly lives on fish fingers and pasta anyway.*
