**1. Geography & Early Days (Pre-1800s)**

*   **Topography:** Hilly! Hampstead Heath, Highgate Hill. This must have been a natural barrier for centuries, slowing down expansion northwards compared to say, the flat lands to the east or west. Good for views, bad for horse-drawn carts.
*   **Forests/Farmland:** Definitely mostly agricultural until relatively recently. Where *exactly* was the edge of London proper? "The City" was the core. North London was literally "the country".
*   **Roman Roads:** Ermine Street (modern A10) and Watling Street (A5) were major routes. Did they influence early settlements? Probably.
    *   *Check:* Where exactly do these run through N. London? A10 goes through Stoke Newington, Tottenham. A5 goes through Edgware, Kilburn. Not directly past *us* but influential.
*   **Villages:** This is key. Most of North London started as distinct villages that got swallowed up.
    *   Islington: Very early commuter village, known for its spas and cattle markets. Close enough to the City.
    *   Highgate: Hilltop village, nice views, attracted wealthier people early on. Cemetery is famous.
    *   Hampstead: Similar to Highgate, health benefits of the air, artists, intellectuals.
    *   Clerkenwell: More industrial, closer to the City.
    *   Stoke Newington, Tottenham, Enfield, Barnet, Finchley, Hendon: Further out, remained more rural for longer.
    *   *Self-note:* Our area... is it a former village, or just land that was developed? Need to pinpoint the nearest 'original' settlement. Will get the Ordnance Survey maps out later. Could be a nice photo project, tracing old boundaries.

**2. The Age of Expansion (19th Century - Victorian Boom)**

This is where things really kicked off. London population exploded, and the need for housing stretched further and further out.

*   **Canals:** Regent's Canal (opened 1820) – major transport artery for goods (coal, timber, bricks) into the heart of London. How did it affect areas like Camden Town, Islington? Probably spurred industrial development along its banks.
    *   *To do:* Cycle along Regent's Canal soon, take some photos of old industrial buildings. Good exercise for my lower back pain too.
*   **Railways:** The absolute game-changer.
    *   North London Railway (NLR): Opened 1850s, linked the docks to Camden, Islington, Hackney. Crucial for goods and passengers. Allowed people to live further out and commute.
    *   Great Northern Railway: King's Cross opened 1852. Main line heading north.
    *   Midland Railway: St Pancras opened 1868. Another major hub.
    *   Impact: Created entirely new suburbs around stations. Property developers bought up land near stations, built houses for commuters. Think about stations like Finsbury Park, Highbury & Islington, Camden Road.
    *   *Question:* What was the social impact? Did it create class segregation? More affluent people moved to leafy suburbs, working class stayed closer to factories or docks.
*   **Trams:** Horse-drawn then electric. Helped fill in the gaps between railway lines. Made more areas accessible.
*   **"Tube" Lines (Underground):** Metropolitan Line (1863) – world's first underground railway. Extended northwards. Eventually the Northern Line, Piccadilly Line.
    *   *Connection:* The Northern Line runs close to us. How old are the stations nearby? This is probably why our area developed when it did. The 'Metroland' phenomenon.

**3. Social & Demographic Shifts**

*   **Class:** North London has always had a mix. Hampstead/Highgate for the wealthy, Islington attracting professionals, but also areas that were more working-class, especially around the industrial hubs (Camden, Tottenham).
*   **Migration:**
    *   Irish immigration (post-famine, railway building).
    *   Jewish communities (late 19th/early 20th century, particularly around Stamford Hill, Golders Green, Hendon).
    *   Caribbean, African, South Asian communities (post-WWII). Claire mentioned that QPA Primary has a really diverse mix of kids, which is brilliant, and reflects this history. Max's nursery, Little Stars, is the same.
    *   Gentification: More recent phenomenon, pushing up house prices (ours included, thankfully for the mortgage with Barclays, but not so much for first-time buyers).

**4. Architecture & Housing (Our House!)**

*   **Georgian (late 18th/early 19th):** Elegant terraces, squares. Think Islington, Canonbury. Brick-fronted, sash windows.
*   **Victorian (mid-late 19th):** Massive variety. Terraced houses, semi-detached for the growing middle class. Bay windows, decorative brickwork, often larger than Georgian.
*   **Edwardian (early 20th):** Still grand, but maybe a bit more restrained than Victorian. Wider, often with more of a 'garden city' feel.
*   **Inter-war (1920s-30s):** This is where our semi-detached probably fits in. Garden suburbs, "mock-Tudor" elements, bay windows, garages becoming common. Built for the burgeoning car-owning middle class (though our 2019 VW Golf is a bit newer!). This era saw huge development along new tube lines.

*   **Post-WWII:** Rebuilding after the Blitz. Some brutalist architecture, council estates.
