**Research Log - Phase 1: Identifying the 'No-Go' List**

**Source 1:** RSPCA website (UK specific, good starting point)
**Keywords:** "plants toxic cats UK", "poisonous houseplants cats"

*   **Lilies (all parts):** **ABSOLUTE NO-GO.** Even pollen can be fatal. This is the big one everyone warns about. I know Claire loves lilies, especially white ones, but they're out. Will need to explicitly tell her, she might try to sneak one in a vase. "No, Claire, not even cut ones. Cat will knock it over, pollen everywhere."
*   **Sago Palm (Cycas revoluta):** Highly toxic, especially seeds. Looks a bit like a mini palm tree. Saw one at Mum's friend's house in Bristol last year. Will need to remember this.
*   **Tulips/Hyacinths (bulbs especially):** We sometimes get these as gifts. Keep any bulbs well away from the cat.
*   **Daffodils (bulbs):** Same as above.
*   **Oleander:** Very toxic. Not common indoors, but good to know.
*   **Azaleas/Rhododendrons:** Toxic. We don't have these, but good general knowledge.
*   **Pothos (Devil's Ivy - *Epipremnum aureum*):** This is a very common one! Lots of people at work have these trailing down shelves. Calcium oxalates – can cause mouth irritation, vomiting. **AVOID.** This is a surprising one. Need to double check, but initially, a strong no.
*   **Philodendrons:** Similar to Pothos, also common. **AVOID.**
*   **Peace Lily (*Spathiphyllum*):** Also contains calcium oxalates. Can cause irritation. **AVOID.** Another popular one. Good job I’m doing this research.
*   **Dieffenbachia (Dumb Cane):** Extremely toxic, can cause severe mouth irritation, swelling, difficulty breathing. **DEFINITELY AVOID.** Saw one of these at Luke's flat in Edinburgh.
*   **Aloe Vera:** Surprisingly, it's toxic. Causes vomiting, lethargy. I have one on my desk in the home office. **MUST RELOCATE/REMOVE.** This is a priority.
*   **Snake Plant (*Sansevieria trifasciata*):** Mildly toxic, can cause nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea. Another common, seemingly indestructible plant. **AVOID if possible, or place completely out of reach.** Max would probably just knock it over.
*   **ZZ Plant (*Zamioculcas zamiifolia*):** Similar to Snake Plant, contains calcium oxalates. Causes irritation. **AVOID.**

**Initial Takeaways from 'No-Go' List:**
A lot of the "easy care" or "popular" houseplants are on the toxic list. This is more complicated than I thought. My initial idea of just grabbing a few random plants from the garden centre is definitely out.

**Research Log - Phase 3: Practical Considerations & Further Questions**

**1. Kitten Behaviour:**
*   **Chewing/Digging:** Kittens are notorious for exploring with their mouths and paws. Even "safe" plants might not be ideal if the kitten consistently tries to eat them (digestive upset is still possible, even if non-toxic) or digs up the soil.
    *   *Action:* Consider deterrents for soil (e.g., pebbles, mesh covers). Plant placement is key – out of immediate reach if possible for floor-standing plants, or hanging baskets for trailing ones.
*   **Watering:** Cats are sometimes attracted to standing water.
    *   *Action:* Ensure no stagnant water in saucers. Water thoroughly but don't overwater.

**2. Soil & Fertilizers:**
*   **Potting Soil:** Generally safe, but some contains additives like perlite or vermiculite which, while non-toxic, aren't digestible. The main concern is the kitten eating *large* quantities of soil (blockage risk).
    *   *Action:* Use a good quality, organic potting mix if possible. Cover exposed soil (pebbles/decorative stones – but make sure they're too large for the kitten to swallow).
*   **Fertilizers:** **MANY FERTILIZERS ARE TOXIC.** Especially granular or liquid chemical ones.
    *   *Action:* **AVOID CHEMICAL FERTILIZERS.** If fertilizing, use organic, cat-safe options *very sparingly* and make sure the plant is watered well afterward to dilute any residue. Better yet, top dress with compost. Keep any fertilizer packets locked away in the utility cupboard with the cleaning supplies (where Max definitely can't get to them).

**3. Pest Control:**
*   **NEVER use chemical pesticides on houseplants if you have pets.** This is a non-negotiable.
    *   *Action:* If plants get pests (e.g., spider mites, aphids), use natural methods:
        *   Wipe leaves with diluted neem oil (some sources say neem is ok, others say not ideal for cats, need to verify. Best to be cautious).
        *   Insecticidal soap (again, check cat safety).
        *   Physical removal (wiping off bugs).
        *   Quarantine new plants for a few weeks to avoid introducing pests.
    *   *Need to check:* Is there a definitive cat-safe insecticidal soap or neem oil brand/method? This is a crucial point for long-term plant health. Maybe just stick to wiping down leaves with plain water or a very mild soapy solution (like fairy liquid, rinsed well).

**4. Plant Sourcing & Placement:**
*   **Where to buy:** Alexandra Palace Garden Centre is usually pretty good for selection and knowledgeable staff. Patch Plants online often lists toxicity information, which is helpful. Claire mentioned wanting to visit the Columbia Road Flower Market one Sunday soon; I could check there.
*   **Placement:**
    *   **Hanging baskets:** Excellent for trailing plants like Spider Plants. Keeps them completely out of reach.
    *   **High shelves:** For smaller plants like African Violets, Haworthia. Make sure the shelf is secure and the cat can't jump up easily (or won't have a clear landing spot if they do). The shelves in the living room (IKEA Kallax unit) are pretty solid.
    *   **Floor-standing:** Areca Palm, Money Tree. Need to ensure the cat can't easily knock them over. Heavy pots are key. Cover the soil.
    *   **My Office:** Aloe Vera needs to go. I can replace it with a Haworthia or African Violet. The office door is usually closed when I'm not in there, but Max sometimes sneaks in.
    *   **Kids' Rooms:** Absolutely no plants in Lily or Max's rooms. Too much risk, and too much mess. Max would probably try to water them with his juice.

**5. Claire's Input:**
*   **Aesthetics:** She will want input on what they *look* like. I need to present her with a shortlist of safe options and let her choose based on style. Maybe show her some pictures from Patch Plants website.
*   **Maintenance:** She's busy with school, and I'm busy with work (and the tropical fish). Plants need to be relatively low maintenance. "Hard to kill" is good.
*   **Budget:** We're already shelling out for the kitten itself, plus all the initial supplies (litter tray, food, toys, scratching post – the one from Pets at Home Claire picked out is enormous). So, reasonable prices for plants.
